## What the screen does

**The role set is the nav row's, exactly.** `RequireRole roles={["TEMPLE_ADMIN",
"KITCHEN_MANAGER", "KITCHEN_STAFF"]}`, matching the reserved
`{ href: "/my-schedule", roles: [ADMIN, MANAGER, KITCHEN] }`. A `VOLUNTEER` is refused by the guard
as well as absent from the menu, which is what D-10 asks for: they hold `VIEW_OWN_SHIFTS` and have no
staff profile for the endpoint to read, and their seva is `/my-shifts`.

**Two sections.** *Next 14 days* — the days ahead the person is actually in, one row each, with hours
— and *Your usual week*, the Mon–Sun template underneath it. The horizon is resolved on the client by
the one rule E6-S1 describes: a per-date exception decides that date, the weekly template decides
every other, nothing else is inferred.

**A day an override turned off is kept in the list**, marked "Changed" with the manager's note.
That is the outbound half of a swap — since E6-S11 the only way the exceptions table can still say
"not working" — and "you are no longer in that Monday" is precisely the change this screen exists to
carry. Ordinary off days are left out, so the list stays a list of when you are in.

**Every date is the temple's day.** The horizon starts at `todayIso()` and steps with the same
local-midnight `addDays` that `/staff-schedule` uses; times go through `hhmm`. No new convention, and
no second copy of the zone.

**Nothing from the employment record is drawn.** The payload is the whole `StaffProfileDetailView`
— it carries date of birth, address and the last four of a PAN, because it is the reader's own record
— and this screen renders only `template` and `exceptions`. There is a test for that.

**The empty states point nowhere the reader is refused.** `/schedule/me` answers 404
`RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (`KMS-400030`) for somebody with no staff profile — a Temple Admin not on the
payroll is the ordinary case — so that is caught by **code, not by status** (per the note on
`ApiError.status`) and answered in words rather than shown as a failure with a code to quote. The
next step is written for whoever is reading it: an admin or manager gets a link to
`/staff-schedule`, which their own role can open; a cook gets a person to ask and **no link at
all**. `/staff-schedule` admits `TEMPLE_ADMIN` and `KITCHEN_MANAGER` only, so linking it for a cook
would have been the T-002 defect again.

## Acceptance criteria

| Criterion | How it was verified |
|---|---|
| Renders the signed-in person's rostered shifts | `lists the days ahead the person is rostered for, with their hours` — Tuesday 8 and Friday 11 September present, `06:00–14:00` rendered. `shows a changed day as changed…` covers the override (12:00–20:00, "Covering the evening", two "Changed" badges). `keeps a day an override turned off…` covers the swap's outbound half and asserts an ordinary off day (Wednesday 9) is **not** listed. `leaves out an override that has already gone` asserts the past exception (5 September) is absent. |
| Works for each of the three admitted roles | `renders for a kitchen manager and a temple admin as well as a cook` renders the page once per role, asserting the heading, a real rostered row, and no "Not your page". Paired with `refuses a volunteer`, which asserts the guard does refuse `VOLUNTEER` — D-10's split, enforced rather than only hidden. |
| Dates and times in the temple's timezone | `starts at the temple's own today, not the device's`. The clock is pinned to `2026-09-07T19:00:00Z`, which is **00:30 on 8 September in Asia/Kolkata** — the device's UTC day and the temple's day are deliberately different dates. The list starts at "Tuesday, 8 September", "Monday, 7 September" is absent, and "Today" marks the right row. The formatters are `todayIso`/`longDay`/`hhmm` from `lib/format`, the same ones `/staff-schedule` uses; no zone is named anywhere in the new file. |
| Empty state offers no destination the reader would be refused at | `tells a cook who to ask, and offers no page they'd be refused at` — the card says "Your kitchen manager sets who works when", and `queryByRole("link")` inside the page's `<main>` finds **nothing**. `offers the roster to somebody who can actually open it` — for a `TEMPLE_ADMIN` the same card carries a link with `href="/staff-schedule"`, a page that role can open. `shows no error code` asserts `KMS-400030` never reaches the screen. Every assertion is scoped to `<main>` via `within()`, because the sidebar carries rows called "Today", "My schedule" and (for an admin) "Staff schedule" and an unscoped query would have been testing the menu. |
| `tsc --noEmit` clean, new vitest passes, `npm run build` succeeds | Sections 1–3 above. |

## Not done / needs Rajeev

**1. Approved leave is not in this payload, and the screen says so.** `/schedule/me` returns the
template and the exceptions only; leave is resolved server-side for the manager's grid
(`ResolvedDay.leaveId`) and is not part of this shape. So a person who has been given Thursday off
still sees Thursday's hours here. I did **not** paper over it client-side: all three roles hold
`REQUEST_OWN_LEAVE`, so I could have fetched `myLeave` and subtracted it, but re-resolving leave in
the browser would put a second answer to "why is this person not in on Thursday" next to the
server's, and the two would eventually disagree. Instead the section carries one muted line —
*"Approved leave is not shown here."* — and the honest fix is a backend one: fold leave into
`scheduleForUser` the way `weekView` already does. **That is outside my contract and is a decision
for you.**

**2. No hand smoke test.** Commandment 5 wants a new user-facing surface pressed by hand. I could
not: five builders share this checkout, `.next/` and a dev server cannot be shared between them, and
signing in as a cook, a manager and an admin in turn needs real Firebase. It wants pressing on
staging after release — open `/my-schedule` as `ikms.kitchen-staff.1`, as a kitchen manager, and as
a Temple Admin who is **not** on the staff register (that last one is the `KMS-400030` empty state,
the branch with the most reasoning behind it and the least real-world exposure so far).

**3. The `/my-shifts` question is untouched, deliberately.** Whether managers and cooks keep that row
is yours to settle, and nothing in this task pre-empts it. Note only that D-10 has already ruled it
goes and `nav.ts` still carries `[VOLUNTEER, MANAGER, KITCHEN]` on it — this task makes the removal
safe rather than making it.

**4. One judgement worth your eye:** the horizon is **14 days**, chosen because "when am I next in,
and has anything moved?" is answered inside a fortnight and a month turns a glanceable list into a
scrolled one. It is one constant (`HORIZON_DAYS`) if you want it longer.
