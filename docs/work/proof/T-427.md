# T-427 — a copy that keeps the temple's own buying setting now says so, on both screens that copy

The import already refused to set `is_not_bought` on an ingredient the temple already owns, and that
refusal is right: copying a recipe is `MANAGE_RECIPES`, which a Kitchen Manager holds, and setting
the buying policy is `MANAGE_BUYING_POLICY`, the Temple Admin's alone. What was wrong is that the
refusal was silent — the names went into the import's audit entry as a raw JSON key and nowhere a
person would look. The copy's response now carries them and both copy screens say so. No error code,
no migration, no permission change; `RecipeImportNotBoughtIT`'s existing thirteen assertions are
untouched and still pass.

## What was built

**Backend.** `RecipeImportService.Imported` gained a fifth component,
`List<String> notBoughtNotApplied`, filled from `resolution.notBoughtNotApplied()` — the same list
the audit entry already carried, produced by `stillBought()`, which is one SQL statement ending
`ORDER BY name`. So the order is stable and it is the temple's own name for each ingredient, not the
book's. `RecipeController`'s response map gained the matching key. Never null, so Jackson always
sends the key and sends `[]` when there is nothing to say.

One deliberate asymmetry, now pinned by a test: the **audit entry still omits the key entirely** when
there is nothing to say (`if (!resolution.notBoughtNotApplied().isEmpty())`), because an entry should
only say what there is to say, while the **response always sends it**, because a screen that had to
tell "absent" from "empty" would get it wrong on the commonest case.

**Frontend.** Both copy screens read it and, only when it is non-empty, show one line.

## The copy

One name:

> **Water stays on your shopping list**
> The library marks it “Not bought”. A Temple Admin can change that in Ingredients.

Three names:

> **Buttermilk, churned, Tender coconut water and Water stay on your shopping list**
> The library marks them “Not bought”. A Temple Admin can change that in Ingredients.

Nothing withheld: no line at all.

**Which guidance shaped it.** The installed `ux-writing` skill was loaded (its four standards:
purposeful, concise, conversational, clear; front-load the important part; 8–14 words a sentence;
name the role rather than writing passively). `DESIGN_SYSTEM.md` §9 *Words* did the rest: sentence
case, one clause per line, **twelve words maximum**, no semicolons, delete anything that restates the
screen. The two body sentences are 6 and 8 words; the one-name title is 6 words.

Specific decisions:

- **The title leads with the consequence**, not with what the software did. "Water stays on your
  shopping list" is the only part the reader has to act on. An earlier draft led with "We kept your
  setting for Water", which puts the software first and the reader's problem second.
- **"Not bought" is the product's own word**, imported as the `NOT_BOUGHT` constant from
  `components/ingredient/IngredientFacts.tsx` rather than retyped — it is the badge on the
  Ingredients list and the label on the ingredient's own checkbox, so the reader is sent to a screen
  that uses the same two words. The consistency rule, and it cannot drift.
- **"A Temple Admin can change that in Ingredients"** names the role instead of instructing the
  reader, because most people copying a recipe are Kitchen Managers and cannot do it. One sentence
  that is right for either reader beats one that is wrong for the commoner one. And it says *open
  Ingredients*, never a path through the menu — a temple can rearrange its own menu now.
- **No "Open Ingredients" button** on the list-screen notice, for the same reason: for the commoner
  reader it would be a button to a screen where they cannot do what it appears to promise.
- **"shopping list"** is the product's word for it (`nav.ts` label, the `<h1>` on `/shopping-list`),
  and it is the exact inverse of how the setting describes itself — "Not bought (water, ice — never
  on a shopping list)".

## Where the message lands on the screen that navigates away, and why

`/recipes/library/[id]` copies and then `router.push`es to the temple's new recipe. A message shown
on the way out is a message nobody reads.

**Chosen:** when — and only when — the copy withheld something, the screen **does not navigate**. The
notice appears under the Add button with a secondary **"Open the recipe"** link to
`/recipes/{new id}`, and the button area becomes "Already in your recipes" so it stops offering a
copy the server would now refuse (KMS-400103). A copy that withheld nothing navigates exactly as
before, and the close-match dialog is now closed by hand where the navigation used to unmount it.

**Rejected:** carrying the names to `/recipes/[id]` in the query string and saying it there. It needs
a third screen this task does not own, and it would say it one step away from the button that caused
it.

**The cost, stated plainly:** one extra click, on the copies that have something to say. That is the
trade I made and it is the one thing on this task a reviewer might decide differently.

On `/recipes` the screen never navigates, so the notice goes in the results grid, `col-span-full`,
**directly under the tile whose plus made the copy**. Not at the top with the two standing notices:
this list runs to four hundred recipes, and a message at the top is off-screen to somebody who
scrolled down to find their dish — which is the same silence T-427 exists to end. Adjacency is also
what lets the copy stay short: it does not have to name the recipe.

## The colour: neutral, and why

`InlineNotice tone="info"`, which renders as `bg-sunken text-ink` — the **neutral** wash, not the
blue `info-bg`. No `autoDismiss`, because there is still something in it for the reader to do.

- Not **green**: `DESIGN_SYSTEM.md:121` reserves `success` for "the confirmation that the reader's
  own action did what they expected". The copy did what they expected; this line is the footnote to
  it, not the confirmation of it.
- Not **amber**: `warning` means "the reader should act, or take care". A temple that genuinely buys
  bottled water has nothing to take care about, and colouring every copy amber would cry wolf.
- Not **red**: nothing is urgent and nothing failed.
- The precedent is on the very setting this is about: `IngredientFacts.tsx` prints the Buying row as
  plain ink in both states with the comment *"a temple that has decided it does not buy water has
  nothing wrong with it"*. The sibling notice on the same Recipes screen ("N ingredients were added
  by a recipe import") reaches the same answer for the same reason.

Asserted in the test on the rendered class, not on the prop: `bg-sunken` present, `bg-success-bg`,
`bg-warning-bg` and `bg-danger-bg` all absent, on both screens.

## Also asked for: what else the import withholds or changes that nobody is told

Reported, not fixed — all of these are outside T-427's scope.

1. **`ingredientsCreated` is on the response and neither screen reads it.**
   `grep -rn "ingredientsCreated\|categoryCreated" frontend/app frontend/components` (excluding
   tests) returns **nothing**. The Recipes list instead asks a separate endpoint
   (`/api/v1/ingredients/library-derived-count`) for a running total; the library recipe's own screen
   says nothing at all and navigates away. So a copy that creates eight new ingredients tells the
   person on the detail screen nothing.
2. **`categoryCreated` — a copy can create a recipe category and nobody is told, anywhere.**
   `resolveCategory` inserts a new `recipe_categories` row on first use, and sets its
   `fasting_compatible` flag from `CategoryMapping.fastingCompatible(categoryKey)` — a real Ekadashi
   setting decided by the book, written into the temple's catalogue, shown on no screen. It is not in
   the `RECIPE_IMPORTED` audit entry either, so it is invisible in both places. Of everything here
   this is the one I would fix next.
3. **The Ekadashi warning exists on only one of the two copy screens.**
   `grep -rln "Check imported ingredients for Ekadashi"` returns `frontend/app/recipes/page.tsx` and
   nothing else. `create()` deliberately leaves every created ingredient unflagged for Ekadashi
   because "the import cannot tell a grain from a spice", and its own comment says the warning box on
   the Recipes page is what makes that honest. Somebody who copies from the library recipe's own
   screen and lands on their new recipe never passes that warning.
4. **An exact or alias match is silent by design.** "Green chillies" in the book goes onto the
   temple's "Green chilli", and "Curd, sour" (now an alias of Curd) goes onto Curd. Correct, and only
   *close* matches are ever asked about — but if the matcher is wrong the recipe is quietly on the
   wrong ingredient and nothing in the response or the audit entry names which lines were matched
   rather than created.
5. **A created ingredient's catalogue unit is set by whichever recipe met it first.** `create()`:
   "an ingredient first met as 200 gm is catalogued in grams, and every later recipe and stock
   movement speaks that unit." Copying a recipe therefore sets a lasting property of the temple's
   catalogue, unreported.
6. **The category name is remapped.** `CategoryMapping.nameFor(categoryKey, bookCategoryName)` can
   file the recipe under a name that is not the one the reader saw on the library screen.
7. **A line's ingredient can differ from the name the book printed.** "Cashew, halved" becomes
   Cashew · halved and "Ginger, peeled" + "Use Ginger" becomes Ginger · peeled. Visible afterwards on
   the recipe, so this is the mildest of the seven, but the copy itself never says it split anything.

## Acceptance criteria

1. **Response carries `notBoughtNotApplied`, always present, `[]` when empty, stable order.** —
   `theResponseNamesWhatItLeftAlone` (present, and identical to the audit entry's list),
   `theResponseSendsAnEmptyListWhenNothingWasKept` (array, empty, key present — and the audit entry
   still omits it, which is the deliberate asymmetry), `threeKeptIngredientsComeBackInNameOrder`
   (written into the recipe in an order neither alphabetical nor its reverse, comes back sorted),
   `aCloseMatchTheTempleUsesIsNamedInTheResponse` (the other route in, by the temple's name).
2. **Nothing withheld ⇒ no extra line on either screen.** — "says nothing extra when the copy
   withheld nothing" and "still opens the temple's new recipe when the copy withheld nothing", plus
   measured in the browser at both widths (`noticePresent: false` after a real clean copy). Both
   assert an absence, so see the note above about the vacuous pass.
3. **One withheld and three withheld each say so, naming them.** — "names the one ingredient it
   kept…", "names three, joined…", and both measured at 1280 and 390 with three long real names,
   nothing clipped.
4. **Both screens say it; the navigating one thought through.** — covered above; the detail screen
   holds still and offers "Open the recipe".
5. **`RecipeImportNotBoughtIT`'s existing assertions pass unchanged.** — no existing test in that
   file was edited; the class went 13 → 17 tests, 0 failures. Under the negative control the same
   thirteen stayed green while only the four new ones went red, which is the proof that the policy
   itself was not touched.
6. **The audit entry still carries the key it carries today.** —
   `anExistingIngredientIsLeftAloneAndTheDisagreementIsRecorded`, `agreementIsNotADisagreement`,
   `theSecondRecipeFindsTheFirstOnesWater` and `aKitchenManagerCopiesWithoutMakingPolicy` all still
   assert on `importedAudit(...)`, unchanged, and pass.
