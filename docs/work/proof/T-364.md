## The separate decision: does a whole ingredient column share a unit?

**No.** Recorded here with the rendered output both ways, and pinned by a test
(`DocumentGenerationIT.anIngredientColumnKeepsEachLineInItsOwnUnit`) so it is not silently reversed
by the next person who reads the one-row-one-unit rule and applies it everywhere.

Fixture: a recipe card holding rice at 10 Kg and cardamom at 0.008 Kg — the case the dispatch named.
Both rendered through `RecipeCardTemplate.render`.

Per figure, which is what the card does today (`recipe-card-column-per-figure.html`):

```html
<tr><td>Rice</td><td class="amt">10 Kg</td></tr>
<tr><td>Cardamom</td><td class="amt">8 gm</td></tr>
```

One unit for the column (`recipe-card-column-one-unit.html`):

```html
<tr><td>Rice</td><td class="amt">10 Kg</td></tr>
<tr><td>Cardamom</td><td class="amt">0.008 Kg</td></tr>
```

**Why the first is the lesser evil.**

The rule is about figures read *together*. A total against the lots it is drawn from, a shortfall
pair, a yield against its base — in each of those the reader is doing arithmetic across the figures,
and a unit that changes in the middle breaks the arithmetic on the page. An ingredient column is not
that. Nobody sums it, nobody subtracts within it, and its rows are not even all of one family: a
single card can hold kilograms, litres and pieces in the same column, so "one unit for the column"
is not even well defined there. Each row is a separate instruction to weigh one thing, and the unit
that makes that instruction easiest to carry out is the unit a person would weigh it in.

The cost of the other choice is measurable and it is the cardamom: `0.008 Kg` is three leading
zeroes on a spice a cook reads as eight grams, on the sheet they are holding at the scale. What it
buys is an alignment nobody is reading down.

**The strongest argument against this, stated fairly:** a column that mixes Kg and gm does make a
reader who *is* scanning down it — to sanity-check a scaled-up recipe, say — re-anchor on each row.
That is a real cost, and it is the same shape of cost as the one this task exists to remove. I am
weighing it lower because the cost falls on a reading nobody has said they do, whereas `0.008 Kg`
degrades the reading the sheet is printed for.

This decision reverses nothing: `DocumentGenerationIT:156-157`, which already pinned a column
reading "5 Kg" beside "500 gm", is untouched and still green.

**The purchase-order sheet** (`DocumentGenerationService.java:227`) was out of scope and the change
does not reach it — it still calls `Quantities.cooks` per line, unmodified. Its lines are a column of
independent buying instructions, so the same reasoning applies; I have deliberately not changed it.

## The rule, and whether it agrees with the landed TypeScript

`Quantities.oneUnitFor(Unit, List<BigDecimal>)` — the ledger form — and
`Quantities.cooksOneUnitFor(...)` — the cook's form, which is what the documents use.

`origin/main` at `1c5e202d` has landed `frontend/lib/format.ts` with `oneUnitFor`. I diffed the
landed file against the copy on `wave-staff-inventory` that I wrote the Java against:

```
$ diff format-branch.ts format-main.ts && echo IDENTICAL
IDENTICAL
```

**Where they agree, exactly:** the unit is the biggest figure's, measured in the family's base;
an all-zero set keeps the stored unit; a unit with no family falls through to the single-figure
renderer; a null figure is an em dash; decimals are the most any figure in the set needs to be said
exactly, floored at three and capped at six. Every one of the eleven cases in the TypeScript
`describe("several figures about one thing, said in one unit")` block is mirrored in
`QuantitiesTest.SetOfFigures` with the same inputs and the same expected strings, including
`oneUnitFor("GM", [4664, 418.2])` → `"0.4182 Kg"` and `oneUnitFor("KG", [500, 0.0004])` →
`"0.0004 Kg"`.

**The one difference, and why a printed page needs it:** `format.ts` has no cook's form of the set
rule, because the screens that needed one unit are ledger screens. A printed document is weighed
against, so `cooksOneUnitFor` adds `roundAsAPersonWould` on top of the identical unit choice —
exactly as `cooks` already adds it to `exact`. Within that, one sub-rule is new and is the heart of
the task: **each figure is rounded at its own scale and then restated in the set's unit.** Rounding
0.008 Kg *as a kilogram figure* would round it to a tenth of a kilo and print "0 Kg" — a lot the
storekeeper is being sent to fetch, reported as nothing. Rounded as the 8 grams it is, and then
written in the row's unit, it prints "0.008 Kg". That sub-rule has no TypeScript twin because there
is no TypeScript cook's-form set renderer to put it in; `QuantitiesTest.SetOfFiguresCooks` says so in
its own javadoc, so if `format.ts` ever grows one, the rule is written down where it will be found.

`frontend/__tests__/quantities.test.ts` needed no change: its eleven cases were already written and
I mirrored them rather than adding to them. Nothing outside my contract had to move.
