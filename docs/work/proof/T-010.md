# T-010 — A vendor invoice can be voided or credited, and a payment reversed

Three acts added, none of which removes anything: a bill can be **struck as never owed** (`VOIDED`,
a third terminal status), **reduced by a credit note** (`credited_amount`, deliberately not a
status), and a payment can be **reversed** with a compensating negative row, which drops an invoice
that had reached `PAID` back to `PENDING` through the sum it already computes.

## The three claims in the brief, checked rather than assumed

1. **`invoice_payments` cannot be marked.** Confirmed: `V40:33` calls `make_append_only`,
   re-registered `V49:75` / `V50:57`; since V49 that is a `BEFORE UPDATE OR DELETE` trigger
   (`reject_append_only_change`) raising `42501` for `current_user = 'kms_app'`. **Tested, not just
   read** — `InvoiceCorrectionIT > the ledger refuses to be marked…` opens one connection as
   `kms_app`, sets `app.tenant_id` on it, proves the row is visible to that session (so the refusal
   is the trigger and not RLS hiding the row), and asserts the `UPDATE` throws.
2. **V40 already designed the reversal and it needs no column for the money.** Confirmed and
   exercised: the compensating row is `amount = -500` against `CHECK (amount <> 0)` and the database
   accepts it. Two columns *were* added, for what the ledger could not already say: `reverses` (which
   payment this undoes) and `reverse_reason`.
3. **The recompute already exists.** Confirmed, and taken further than "reuse it": the decision moved
   out of `InvoicePaymentService.recordPayment` into **`VendorInvoiceService.restateStatus`**, the one
   place that now decides `PAID`, called by all three write paths (payment, reversal, credit). Three
   callers each deciding "is this settled now" is the `PurchaseOrderLineView::subject` failure again.

## Acceptance criteria

1. **Reversing a payment that took an invoice to `PAID` returns it to `PENDING`, on the real database
   under RLS.** `InvoiceCorrectionIT > reversing the payment that paid a bill puts the bill back in
   the queue` — pays ₹500 on a ₹500 bill, asserts `PAID`, reverses, asserts `PENDING` and
   `SUM(amount) = 0`, all read straight from PostgreSQL.
2. **The original row still exists, unmodified, and the compensating row references it.** Asserted
   against the database, not the API: `row_to_json(p)` is captured before the reversal and compared
   byte-for-byte after (the *whole* row, including the columns V103 added, rather than the columns
   the test happened to think of), then the correction is read back with `amount = -500`,
   `reverses = <payment>`, `reverse_reason`, and `count(*) = 2` for the invoice.
3. **A second reversal answers `KMS-400133`; a second void answers `KMS-400132`.** Both tested, and
   both check the *first* act survived — the second void asserts `void_reason` is still the first
   reason, which is the whole reason a 409 was chosen over a silent return. A database-level
   `UNIQUE INDEX invoice_payments_one_reversal_each ON (reverses) WHERE reverses IS NOT NULL` closes
   the race the read-then-write guard cannot: nothing about the original row changes when it is
   reversed, so no row lock exists to serialise two administrators pressing at once.
4. **A voided invoice leaves the payables list.** `a voided bill leaves the payables list…` asserts
   `outstanding = 700` before and `$.length() = 0` after. It works because `VOIDED` is a *status*:
   `InvoicePaymentService.payables`' existing `WHERE vi.status = 'PENDING'` skips it untouched.
   **Sweep of every consumer of `vendor_invoices.status` in the package**, as asked: `payables`
   (`:106`), `VendorInvoiceService.list`'s status and overdue filters, and the derived `overdue`
   expression in `SELECT` — all keyed on `'PENDING'`, so all correct for a struck bill without
   change. One consumer *outside* the package is wrong; see "Needs Rajeev" below.
5. **The voided / credited state is visible on the screen, not only in the API.** Detail page: a
   `Voided` badge, a warning notice carrying the reason and the moment, both correction buttons
   withdrawn, and — for a credit — a `Credited` figure with `Owed after credit` beside it. List page:
   a `Voided` badge and a `Voided` filter option, and `less ₹400 credited` beside an amount that no
   longer means what is owed. Six of the ten frontend tests assert exactly this.
6. **All three acts audited under the right action.** `all three acts are audited…` asserts one
   `INVOICE_CREDITED`, one `INVOICE_VOIDED`, one `INVOICE_PAYMENT_VOIDED` — **and asserts the cross
   pair is zero**, because the doc comment's distinction (a void says never owed, a credit says owed
   and now owed less) is worth nothing if the two are interchangeable in the trail. It also asserts
   the stored `after_state ->> 'status'` is `VOIDED`: every after-state here is **read back from the
   row** rather than built from the request, per the wave 4b lesson.

## Rulings I was asked about

- **The 409, against `StaffPayService.voidPayment`'s silent idempotence: I agree, and it is the
  reason `KMS-400132` bought something.** A body-less void is a double-click; a void carrying a
  reason is a second act, and the test asserts the first reason still stands afterwards. Swallowing
  the second call would discard what the admin typed and leave them believing it was recorded.
- **`reverseInvoicePayment` over `voidInvoicePayment`: I agree, and would have argued for it.** The
  table forbids a mark, and a URL saying `/void` promises one. The IT proves the promise would have
  been a lie: the `UPDATE` raises `42501`.

## Decisions I made that were not in the brief

- **A credit note is a running total on the invoice, not a table of credit notes.** Each credit's
  amount, reason, author and date are kept by the audit trail under `INVOICE_CREDITED`, which is this
  project's record of who did what and why. `api.ts` exposes only a total, nothing in the product
  lists credit notes one by one, and a table nobody reads is where two accounts of one fact drift
  apart. Reversible later if a credit-note register is ever wanted.
- **A credit reduces what is owed, so it recomputes the status.** Otherwise a ₹1,000 bill with ₹600
  paid and ₹400 credited can never be settled — it would sit in the payables queue for ever waiting
  for ₹400 nobody owes. `restateStatus` therefore compares payments against `amount - credited_amount`,
  and so does the overpayment guard in `recordPayment` and the `outstanding` on the payables view.
  With no credits these are arithmetically identical to before, which is why every existing test is
  untouched and green.
- **A credit that would take what is owed below what has already been paid is refused**
  (`VALIDATION_FAILED`, naming the field and the maximum). That situation is a vendor *refund*, not a
  credit note, and this product has no record to put one in. See "Needs Rajeev".
- **Paying a `VOIDED` bill is refused with `KMS-400132`**, and a reversal on a voided bill is
  *allowed* but does not resurrect it (`restateStatus` treats `VOIDED` as terminal). Recovering money
  from a bill that should never have been raised is legitimate; putting that bill back in front of
  somebody to pay a second time is not.
- **Voiding a bill that already has payments is allowed**, with the paid-to-date recorded in the
  audit entry. There is no reserved code for refusing it and I will not mint one. See below.
- **The compensating row is dated the same day as the payment it undoes.** A reversal carrying
  today's date would show a monthly spend figure with money paid in July and returned in September,
  neither of which happened; when the correction was *made* is `created_at`, already stored.

## Not done / needs Rajeev

- **One consumer of `vendor_invoices` outside my contract is now wrong, and I did not touch it.**
  `backend/src/main/java/org/iskcon/kms/donation/GivingPageController.java:153` computes the public
  giving page's "what one plate costs" as `SELECT COALESCE(SUM(amount),0) FROM vendor_invoices WHERE
  invoice_date >= CURRENT_DATE - INTERVAL '30 days'` — no status filter and no credit subtraction, so
  a struck or credited bill inflates a figure **shown to donors**. The fix is one clause
  (`AND status <> 'VOIDED'`, summing `amount - credited_amount`), but `donation/` is explicitly not
  mine. Needs a task of its own.
- **A vendor refund has nowhere to live.** The credit guard above refuses the case where a vendor
  reduces a bill the temple has already overpaid. That is a real situation and the honest answer is a
  refund record, which is a product decision rather than a defect.
- **No error code exists for "this bill has payments against it".** Voiding such a bill is currently
  allowed. If Rajeev would rather it were refused until the payments are reversed, that needs a new
  `KMS-` code, which I am not permitted to take.
- **One deliberate departure from a reserved doc comment.** `AuditAction.INVOICE_PAYMENT_VOIDED` says
  "its entity is the compensating row". I record the entity as **the invoice** (`VENDOR_INVOICE` /
  invoice id), matching `INVOICE_PAYMENT_RECORDED`, and name both payment rows in the after-state
  (`paymentId`, `reversalId`). Reason: there is no `INVOICE_PAYMENT` entity type, so filing a payment
  id under `VENDOR_INVOICE` would put an id in `entity_id` that matches no invoice and break the
  per-bill drill-in — an audit trail that cannot be followed. The doc comment's actual purpose ("a
  reader who filters on it sees corrections and not the payments they correct") is satisfied by the
  action, which is what the filter uses. Flagging it rather than quietly differing.
- **No hand smoke-test.** I have not driven the deployed application as a Temple Admin — this is
  unmerged, undeployed work in a shared checkout, and I do not commit or deploy. Commandment 5's
  manual pass on the two new panels (void, credit note, reverse) still needs doing on staging once
  this ships.
