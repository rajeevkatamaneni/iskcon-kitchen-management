## What I decided, and why

**The bound is a row cap of 50, not a months-back window.** Both bound the query; they fail
differently. A window is bounded by the calendar rather than by the data, so a devotee who serves
only at festivals sees three rows from "the last twelve months" while one who serves twice a week
sees a hundred — it does nothing for the person it was written for and hides history from the
person it was not. A row cap is the same size for everyone and is the number the response is
actually made of. It is `SignupService.PAST_SHIFTS_LIMIT` and `HISTORY_CAP` in `page.tsx`, each
naming the other. The screen says **"Showing your 50 most recent shifts."** when fifty arrive,
because fifty shown silently to a volunteer of three years is a falsehood by omission.

**The response shape is the brief's, unchanged.** I kept `source` and `signedUpAt` even though only
`source` is rendered (as the same "From waitlist" badge the upcoming rows carry). The row is
`MyShiftView` plus the two attendance fields, and keeping the two shapes identical is worth more
than saving a field: it is the same signup read afterwards.

**The history predicate is `myShifts`'s, negated.** `shift_date < CURRENT_DATE`, `released_at IS
NULL`, `status = 'OPEN'`. Both sides use the database's `CURRENT_DATE`, so the two lists partition
her signups exactly — nothing appears twice, nothing falls between them. A test asserts both halves
against the same three rows.

**Removals and cancellations are not folded in**, as instructed. `/my-shifts/released` already
answers those and the screen names each section for what it is.

**The three attendance words are the coordinator's, the colour is not.** "Came", "Did not come",
"Not marked" — copied from the roster (`app/volunteers/[id]/page.tsx:368` and around it) so one
label means one fact everywhere. The roster paints "Did not come" `text-warning`; here all three are
the neutral `bg-sunken`/`text-ink-secondary` chip. Amber on the roster is a real warning — a spot
went uncovered and a coordinator may need to act. On the page of the person it is about it is none
of those: nothing asks her to act, she cannot change the mark, and an amber flag beside her own name
reads as the app telling her off. Measured colour of the rendered chip: `rgb(79, 81, 84)` on
`rgb(241, 242, 243)`. A test pins that the class carries no `warning|danger|success`.

**`attended` is read with `getObject`, never `getBoolean`.** `ResultSet.getBoolean` returns `false`
for SQL NULL, which would turn every unmarked shift into a recorded absence — on the page of the
person it would accuse. Both versions compile; the negative control below proves the test sees the
difference.

**Migration V159 adds `shift_signups (tenant_id, volunteer_user_id)`.** Both existing indexes lead
with `shift_id` (V34:62,64), so no index could answer "which shifts am I on" and every read of a
person's own roster was a sequential scan. V107 refused an index for a query nobody had written;
this one is for three queries that exist — `myShifts`, `myPastShifts`, `myReleasedShifts`. Applied
and present on the local database:


## Acceptance criteria

**1. She sees her own past services as well as what is coming, with what she signed up for and
whether she turned up.** Verified in the browser, signed in as
`ikms.volunteer.1@trading4good.org` against the seeded temple. Nine past rows under **Past shifts**,
three upcoming above, each past row carrying title, day, hours, place and the mark. The live JSON is
above.

**2. An unmarked shift reads as unmarked, never as an absence.** Three ways.
- Server: the live response carries `"attended": null` for the 4 Sept dinner service (above).
- Screen: measured in the DOM at 1280 —
  `marks: ["Came","Came","Came","Did not come","Did not come","Did not come","Not marked","Came","Came"]`.

  browser call from the web on `:3003` failed the CORS preflight with `403 Invalid CORS request` —
  the screen just said "We can't reach the server". That is a gap in the stack script, not in
  anything I built, and it would have blocked any builder trying to drive the local UI. The running
  API now allows `:3003`; the script still does not, so the next restart from the script reinstates
