# T-431 — the whole-number refusal says why, in every box

The browser now gives the reason a fraction was refused, in the server's own words, at all 26
counted quantity boxes:

> **Agarbatti is counted in whole pieces**

instead of

> ~~Tell me when Agarbatti drops below must be a whole number~~

---

## What was decided, and where it departs from the brief

**The sentence is the reason clause only.** The server says *"Apron is counted in whole pieces.
Enter 88 or 89."* (`IngredientUnits.java:182-201`). The brief said to take the server's sentence
"minus the full stop". I took the first clause and dropped "Enter 7 or 8", because:

- `DESIGN_SYSTEM` §9 is "One clause per line of copy. Twelve words maximum." Keeping both halves
  means two clauses and an internal full stop under a field, which §9 also says is "almost never".
- The `ux-writing` skill's own numbers: eight words or fewer is the 100% comprehension band.
  "Agarbatti is counted in whole pieces" is six.
- It has to fit 26 boxes, several of them in a table cell that `RecordDeliveryPanel`'s own comment
  measures at **154px on a phone**. Measured text width at the app's real font and size
  (`12px/16px Anek Latin`): the reason clause alone is **179px**; with "Enter 7 or 8" after it, it
  is a third and fourth wrapped line in that cell.

The reason is the half that was missing. The fix follows from it, and the box already carries
`step="1"` and `inputMode="numeric"`.

**`better-writing` argues the other way** and should be recorded as doing so: "an error is an
instruction", and this is a statement of fact. If Rajeev wants the instruction too, the change is
one line in `wholeNumber` and it needs the typed value plumbed as a third fact. Worth his call.

**Pack-counting boxes do not claim pieces.** Two boxes carry `step="1"` because they count packs,
not because of the unit — `PurchaseOrderEditor.tsx:418` and `orders/new/page.tsx:604`, both
`l.pack ? "1" : stepForUnit(l.unit)`. The ingredient inside the bag may be measured in kilograms,
so "Rice is counted in whole Kg" would be flatly false. They say **"Rice, Sona Masoori is ordered
in whole packs"**. "Whole packs" is the product's own phrase, already in `PACK_SIZES_HINT` on the
ingredient screen ("The shopping list suggests whole packs").

**The brief mis-grouped three boxes, and I did not follow it there.** It lists the two "Size" boxes
— now `InvoiceItemsTable.tsx:587` and `ingredient/PackSizes.tsx:175`, and by the same logic the
third at `vendors/[id]/page.tsx:860` — as pack-counting. They are not. Their own comments say what
they are:
*"How much is in one pack, in the unit chosen beside it. A pack of 12.5 pieces is not a pack of
anything."* The subject there is the thing inside the pack, so the unit reason is the true one and
those three say "Agarbatti is counted in whole pieces".

**The fallback is exactly today's sentence**, whenever either fact is missing — a form where no
ingredient has been chosen, a recipe nobody has named yet. An invented subject ("This is counted in
whole pieces") would be worse than the label. Two rows of the table below show it.

**One defect found while building, fixed.** On the meal recording form the two boxes on a row
(cooked, served) had their sentences joined with " · ". They used to differ because each named its
own box; now they are the same reason, so the row printed *"Ladoo is counted in whole pieces · Ladoo
is counted in whole pieces"*. It is de-duplicated — `MealServices.tsx:925`.

## How the plumbing works

`wholeNumber(name, counted?)` is the one place the words live. Two entry points reach it:

- **Inside `<Form>` (18 boxes).** The box carries `{...countedBox(subject, unit)}`, which writes
  `data-counted-subject` and `data-counted-unit` (or neither). `Form.tsx`'s `factsOf` reads them
  into `ControlFacts.counted`, and `messageFor`'s `stepMismatch` branch passes it through.
- **Outside `<Form>` (8 boxes, 5 screens).** `wholeNumberProblem(name, step, value, counted?)`.

The `step` still decides *whether* a box refuses; `counted.unit` only decides what the sentence
calls the thing. They are deliberately two facts, because a pack box carries `step="1"` and a
`counted.unit` of `PACKS`. The sentence also re-asks `isCountedUnit`, so a `step="1"` written by
hand on a weighed box cannot produce "Rice is counted in whole Kg".

### What else in the product can still hold a fraction of a counted thing

Nearly all of it is deliberate and documented, and one thing is not. The deliberate ones: recipe
scaling rounds a planned amount **up** rather than refusing, so a plan for 137 people of a recipe
measured in ladoos gives whole ladoos by rounding, not by rejection; `CONSUMPTION`, `ISSUE` and
`USED_BEYOND_RECORDED_STOCK` movements carry figures the application worked out rather than figures
a person typed, and a FEFO draw across batches genuinely takes 2.5 from one lot and the rest from
the next, so forcing those whole would make the ledger disagree with the shelf; `compensate`
reverses a past movement exactly as it was written, on purpose, because a reversal that re-validated
could not undo something written before the rule existed; and a pack count is meant to be
fractional at the gate — `RecordDeliveryPanel`'s own comment records that part of a bag really does
come off a van, and the rule there lands on the **stock** amount instead. The one place that is
neither enforced nor documented as intentional is the **reorder threshold**: the request records
carry only `@PositiveOrZero`, the service writes the value with no `requireWhole` and no unit
lookup, the column is `NUMERIC(14,3)` with a `>= 0` CHECK and nothing else, and
`PartOfACountedThingIT` has no threshold case at all despite its preamble claiming it covers "every
door a person or the API can enter one". The survey the brief carried is correct, and the API call
above is the proof.

**Not built, by instruction.** The fix belongs to the next wave's task that is already rewriting
that field to carry a unit.
