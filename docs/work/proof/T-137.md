# T-137 — the vendor's lead time is one promise, computed once, enforced on Mark sent and shown wherever it matters

The sum is **not built three times**. `LeadTimes.orderBy` and `OrderUrgency.on` (T-090) stay the only
implementations; T-137 adds one method to `LeadTimes` that answers *which* lead time governs an
order, one small record that carries their answer around, and callers. Nothing on any screen
subtracts a date from another date — the server sends the order-by date, the zone and the verdict,
and the screens choose words.

**The planner badge is untouched and still agrees.** `recordedByIngredient()`, `effectiveDays()`,
`orderBy()` and `OrderUrgency` are byte-for-byte as T-090 left them; `ShoppingListIT` and
`SufficiencyIT` were run for that reason and pass.

## What was built, and the decisions inside it

### The one sum, and where it now lives

`LeadTimes.governingByPurchaseOrder(ids)` answers *which* lead time an order is measured by: the
**longest** recorded against **that order's own vendor** for the ingredients on it. Rajeev's answer,
asked directly — the order is only deliverable when its slowest item is. `MAX` ignores nulls, so a
vendor who has answered for the rice and not the jaggery governs the order by the rice.

`OrderLeadTime.beforeSending(...)` / `.asSent(...)` then call `LeadTimes.orderBy` and
`OrderUrgency.on` — the T-090 originals — and nothing else computes anything. Every screen and the
gate read the result off `PurchaseOrderView`.

**A draft is measured live; a sent order is read off its own row.** That split *is* the
no-retroactive rule, and it is one `if` in `PurchaseOrderService.withLeadTimes`.

### The gate, on Mark sent

`POST /purchase-orders/{id}/send` refuses with **KMS-400148** when the order is past its order-by
date, and takes `{"sendAnyway": true}` as the override. The screen shows the refusal and then a
separate **Send it anyway** button — a second deliberate press, because *"That is a FAVOR we are
asking"* and a rule that made it impossible would only teach people to write a date they do not mean.

The middle zone (the optional nudge) is built: it is one branch of the same line, so it cost nothing
and complicated nothing. It is an amber badge and no gate at all.

### The stamp

`send` writes `lead_time_days` and `sent_after_lead_time` in the same UPDATE as `sent_at`. The
scorecard reads the boolean and does **no date arithmetic about lead times at all**. That is what
makes an edit to a vendor's profile unable to re-judge an order already placed, and it is the claim
Control 2 below tests directly.

The lead time is stamped **whether or not the order was late** — it is the figure that applied, and a
reader asking why an on-time delivery counted needs it as much as one asking why a late order was
excused.

### The scorecard

An order with `sent_after_lead_time` is counted in `ordersPlaced` and judged nowhere: it is checked
**before** the abandoned tick, so a no-show on a late-sent order counts against nobody even where one
exists — an order cancelled that way before this task shipped, since V125 adds no constraint over
stored rows.
`ordersSentLate` sits beside the percentage as a neutral pill reading *"2 orders we sent late — not
counted"*, and the caveat paragraph explains it. Neutral and not amber: the order says nothing bad
about the vendor.

**The fill rate deliberately does not move.** Ordering late excuses *lateness*; it does not excuse a
half-empty lorry, and T-124's argument against silently changing an existing percentage applies. It
is flagged for Rajeev below rather than inferred.

### The draft nobody sends

`PurchaseOrderAutoCancelJob` → `PurchaseOrderAutoCancelRunner.sweep()` → per tenant,
`PurchaseOrderService.autoCancelAbandonedDrafts()`. Daily at 04:30 IST. `status = 'DRAFT'` only, so
D-26's part-delivered order is structurally out of reach. Cancel reason is **"Past need by date"**,
Rajeev's own words; `auto_cancelled` is what makes the screen say **Auto Cancelled**.

**The loop closes, and I checked rather than assumed it:** `ShoppingListService`'s coverage query is
`WHERE po.status IN ('DRAFT', 'SENT')` (`ShoppingListService.java:469`), so a cancelled order stops
covering its ingredients and the lines come back on the next read of the derived list.

**Why a column and not a sixth status:** every query that groups or filters on `status` — the
scorecard's `LIVE_ORDER`, the invoice screen's open list, the order filter — would have stopped
seeing those orders silently, and the breakage would look like a smaller number rather than an error.

## The rule that was living in a form, and now is not

**`KMS-400149` refuses the "Vendor Never Delivered this Order" tick on an order we sent after the
notice that vendor asked for.** I first built this as unoffered-and-inert — the form hides the box,
and the scorecard excludes the whole order so a tick carries no weight anywhere — flagged it as a
gap, and was correctly sent back. **The project had already decided this question and the decision
was sitting three lines above my own code**, in `PO_NEVER_SENT_TO_VENDOR`'s comment: *"The screen
hides the box; this is what stops it anyway. A rule that lives only in a form is not a rule — the
same endpoint takes the same field from anything that can post to it."* And D-25 makes it a rule
rather than a courtesy: **we asked late, so putting our own lateness on a supplier's permanent record
is the specific thing the ruling forbids**, whatever the percentage happens to do with it afterwards.

The two guards now sit together in `cancel` and are one idea said twice — nothing was asked of this
vendor, or nothing was asked of them *in time*. Both read a column stamped in exactly one place by
`send`, so each question has one answer and not two.

**Tested the way T-129's is: by posting the field straight at the endpoint**, never through the
screen — `aLateSentOrderCannotBlameTheVendor`, which also asserts the order is *still SENT* after the
refusal and that the ordinary cancellation (without the tick) still succeeds: it is the claim that is
refused, never the act. Plus the opposite case, `anOrderSentInTimeCanStillBlameTheVendor`, because a
guard that refused every no-show would pass the first test while quietly destroying T-124.

## The second answer, removed

**`leadTimeWarning` compared every needed-by date against a hard-coded two days** —
*"Sooner than the 2 days a vendor usually gets"* — which was honest before T-090 stored a real figure
per vendor and ingredient, and is a second answer to the question this task exists to make single.
Inside my original contract I could only put the vendor's real number *beside* it, which is two
numbers on one screen: the form saying "2 days" while the server had just refused the send against
five. **The coordinator granted `lib/format.ts` and its test mid-task**, having checked that
`PurchaseOrderEditor.tsx` (which I already held) and that test file are its only callers in the tree.

So it takes the governing lead time now, and `LEAD_BUFFER_DAYS` is **deleted** — nothing else in the
frontend referenced it. Three things fell out of the rewrite and are worth stating because each is a
rule rather than a detail:

- **Null is silence, not two days.** A vendor who has never given a lead time has no cutoff, so there
  is nothing to warn about. The old default invented a promise on a supplier's behalf.
- **Zero is cash and carry** and warns about nothing.
- **The parameter is required-and-nullable, not optional with a default**, which is `lib/api.ts`'s own
  convention and earns its place here: a default would let a caller that *has* the vendor's figure
  forget to pass it and get a silence indistinguishable from a vendor nobody has asked.

The editor also stops printing "Heritage Fresh Dairy asked for 2 days' notice" underneath the warning
when the warning is showing, because the warning now names the same number and one fact in two shapes
is what this whole task is against.

## The reservation I did not need, and why

**An `AuditAction` constant for the auto-cancellation.** **I was told to expect this one and it turns
out it could not have been used.** The brief suspected the sweep needed its own constant, *"because a
scheduled job cancelling somebody's order is exactly the act a reader of the log comes looking
for"* — which is right about the reader and wrong about the mechanism.

`audit_events.actor_user_id` is `NOT NULL` (V3:71) and `AuditService.write` reads the actor's id,
name and role off a verified user. **There is no system principal in this application**, so there is
nothing for a constant to name as the actor, and inventing one inside this task would be a new shared
concept smuggled in as a side effect of a feature.

So the sweep writes no audit row and instead leaves a line on the order's own append-only trail
(`po_events`, event type `AUTO_CANCELLED`) **with no actor** — which is how the trail says the system
acted rather than naming somebody who was asleep. That trail is what the order screen shows, so the
act is legible exactly where a reader will look for it. `aDraftNobodySentIsSweptAway` asserts the
null actor deliberately, because an absence nobody asserts is an absence the next person will
"fix".

## RLS

`purchase_orders` is under `enable_tenant_rls()` (V26:53). **V125 touches no rows** — three
`ADD COLUMN`s and three `ADD CONSTRAINT`s, all DDL, run as the table owner — so there is no tenant
loop, and that is a decision, not an oversight: every existing order correctly reads "no recorded
lead time, not late, not auto-cancelled", and back-filling `lead_time_days` from today's
`vendor_supplies` would be exactly the retroactive judgement the rule forbids. The file says so.

The **sweep** does adopt each tenant in turn (`PurchaseOrderAutoCancelRunner`), because it writes
rows: without it the UPDATE would run with `app.tenant_id` unset, match nothing through the policy's
`NULLIF`, and report a cheerful zero. `PurchaseOrderLeadTimeIT` drives the runner, so the sweep is
exercised through the same unprivileged path the application uses.

## Acceptance criteria

**1. One implementation of the sum.** `LeadTimes.orderBy` and `OrderUrgency.on` are unchanged from
T-090 and are the only places the subtraction and the comparison happen. `LeadTimes` gained one
method that answers *which* lead time governs an order; `OrderLeadTime` carries the answer. No screen
does date arithmetic — verified by `does not decide for itself: a draft the dates would call late
shows what the server said`, which hands the order screen a fixture whose dates scream "late" and a
server verdict of `IN_TIME`, and asserts the screen shows the server's answer.

**2. The planner badge still agrees.** Nothing it reads was modified. `ShoppingListIT`,
`ShoppingListStatementCountIT` and `SufficiencyIT` pass unchanged in the run above. **No finding to
report: the badge's output does not change.**

**3. Mark sent is the gate, with the three zones.** `insideTheLeadTimeIsSilent` (11 Sept),
`theLastDayIsANudge` (13 Sept), `pastTheCutoffIsRefusedUntilSomebodyMeansIt` (14 Sept) — Rajeev's own
example, with Heritage Fresh Dairy and two days. The third asserts KMS-400148, the exact message, that
the order is **still DRAFT** after the refusal, and that the second press with `{"sendAnyway":true}`
succeeds.

**4. The cutoff is a date, not a time.** `LeadTimes.orderBy(neededBy, days)` is `LocalDate.minusDays`
and `OrderUrgency.on` compares `LocalDate`s. No time of day is added to `needed_by` anywhere in this
change.

**5. The warning says why it matters.** KMS-400148's own words, plus the sentence beside the override
button — *"A late delivery on it won't count against them"* — and the trail line on the order:

no-show tick when cancelling it`, with its opposite `offers the no-show tick on an order we did send
in time`. On the server it is **KMS-400149**, asserted by posting the field straight at the endpoint
(`aLateSentOrderCannotBlameTheVendor`), because a rule that lives only in a form is not a rule.

**8. The exclusions are visible on the scorecard.** `ordersSentLate` on the row and on the total, as
