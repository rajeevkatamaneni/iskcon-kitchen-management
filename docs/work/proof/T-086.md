## What I counted as committed, and why

`CommittedStockService` sums the scaled recipe requirements of meal plans that pass **three**
filters. Each exclusion has a different reason and getting any one of them wrong makes every figure
on the screen wrong.

1. **`status = 'PLANNED'` only.** This is the double-subtraction guard. A recorded meal is `COOKED`
   and recording it already posted `CONSUMPTION` movements, so its rice has *left the on-hand
   figure*. Counting the plan as well subtracts the same rice twice. `CANCELLED` covers both a plan
   called off before cooking and a `notMade` dish — `ServedMealService` writes `CANCELLED` with
   `not_made` beside it — and neither will ever draw anything.
2. **From the temple's today forward.** A past-dated plan still sitting at `PLANNED` has *not* drawn
   its stock, so counting it would not double-subtract — but it is a **recording gap**, not a claim
   on the shelf, and it never resolves itself. Left in, every day nobody records shaves availability
   again, permanently. That gap is a real problem and it is T-087's.
3. **Within the ordering horizon** — 14 days, extended to reach any festival resolving within 30.
   **Not a new number:** it is exactly the window `SufficiencyService.shortfallFeed()` already buys
   against. The argument for bounding at all is that *available* means "what is left of what is on
   the shelf, for the meals that will actually draw on it". A Janmashtami plan three months out will
   be cooked from rice nobody has bought yet; subtracting it from today's sack says "you are short"
   when the true answer is "you will buy it", and it would leave every staple permanently red — the
   unbounded horizon making *available* meaningless, which the brief asked me to weigh. As the
   festival comes inside thirty days its claim appears, which is the same moment the shopping list
   starts buying for it. The two screens then agree because they read the same window.

Two smaller decisions worth naming:

- **A recipe naming one ingredient twice merges into one claim per dish**, so the detail list shows a
  meal once and nobody adds the same dish up twice.
- **`available < 0` is Low regardless of the reorder level.** There is no reading of "we have
  promised more of this than we hold" that is fine, and an item with no threshold set — the
  commonest case in a temple that has just started tracking — would otherwise read *Fine* while
  over-promised. The **table** still shows only Fine/Low, exactly as he settled it; the **detail
  page's** badge names which of the two it is ("More committed than you hold" vs "Below reorder
  level"), because there it has room and the two are fixed in different places.

**Prior art read first, as instructed:** `SufficiencyService.evaluate()` allocates *batch* stock to
meals in planning order. I did not change it — T-088 is the task that points it at *available* — and

## Migration: not needed, and verified rather than assumed

Committed is derived from `meal_plans` + `recipe_ingredients`, both of which already exist and both
of which already carry `enable_tenant_rls()`. No column, no table, no `CHECK`. Nothing is
materialised: the committed and available figures are computed on every read, the same way `onHand`
always has been. The test `available is on hand minus committed, and is derived rather than stored`
proves it the only way that means anything — it cancels the plan and watches `available` go back to
50 kg **with the stock-movement count still at 1**, which a stored figure could not do.

Performance: one indexed query over `meal_plans` per list request (not per row), and recipe scaling
memoised on `(recipeId, targetYield)` so a fortnight of the same khichadi is one scale, not fifteen.
The list screen's whole committed pass is one extra query plus the distinct recipes in the horizon.
I did not need to materialise anything and am not asking to.

## How the detail page presents the committed list

Under the header, a card of three figures — **Committed**, **Available**, **Reorder level** — sits
beside the large *On hand*. That is where the reorder level went when the column came off the table,
and four numbers next to each other answer "why does this say Low" without a sentence of
explanation.

Below the batch table, a section **"Committed to meals — soonest first"**, in planning order, with
one row per dish: **Day** (a link to `/planner/<ISO date>`), **Meal** (the event's own name where it
has one, its kind otherwise), **Dish**, **Claims** (the quantity, in the item's own unit). A line
above it says what window is counted and why a plan beyond it is not subtracted. Empty state:
"Nothing in the plan draws on this yet."
