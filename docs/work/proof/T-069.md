# T-069 — a struck gift stops buying wish-list items

Wave 7's donation void (V104, T-012) marks a voided gift with a `voided_at` column rather than a
status value, deliberately: the row has to stay readable one at a time by an administrator
answering "what did Govind Das give us in August". The cost of that choice is that a struck gift
still has `status = 'COMPLETED'`, so every sum in the repo that filtered on status alone went on
spending money the temple had already decided it never received. Three such sums funded wish-list
items. All three now exclude a struck gift.

## The change

Three SQL predicates, and nothing else. Each carries a comment explaining the mark-versus-status
reasoning, because the next person to read one of these sums will not have V104's header in front of
them.

`WishlistService.markFulfilledIfComplete` and the `SELECT` constant's `paid_inr`:

```sql
WHERE d.wishlist_item_id = i.id AND d.status = 'COMPLETED'
  AND d.voided_at IS NULL
```

`MonetaryDonationService.completedAmount`:

```sql
WHERE wishlist_item_id = ? AND status = 'COMPLETED' AND voided_at IS NULL
```

No fulfilment logic changed. No status transition changed. The `status = 'ACTIVE'` guard on the
flip is untouched — see the deferred section.

## The question the coordinator asked: does my fix leave a visibly wrong row?

**Yes, and the expectation put to me was right — but it is worse than expected in one respect and
better in another.** Measured, not assumed:

| | before the void | after the void |
|---|---|---|
| `wishlist_items.status` | `FULFILLED` | `FULFILLED` |
| `paid_inr` (what the API returns) | `15000.00` | `0` |
| price × quantity | ₹15,000 | ₹15,000 |

So the row reads **FULFILLED beside ₹0 of ₹15,000**. This is created by my fix: before it, the row
was internally consistent and wrong; after it, it is visibly inconsistent. That is still the right
trade — the alternative is a temple that believes a grinder arrived — but Rajeev should see it here
rather than on a screen.

**Is it visible to a donor? Yes.** `WishlistService.forGiving()` (the method the brief called
`listPublic`; it was renamed on 2026-08-29 when giving stopped being possible without an account)
selects `WHERE i.status IN ('ACTIVE', 'FULFILLED')`. The contradictory row therefore appears on the
page devotees give from, not only on an admin screen. `anAlreadyFulfilledItemIsLeftContradictory`
asserts the item is in `forGiving()`'s result after the void.

**And pressing "Give" on it is refused.** This is the extra dimension, and it is the sharpest edge.
`startWishlistCheckout` refuses when the item's status is not `ACTIVE`, *before* it ever computes
what is owed (`MonetaryDonationService:105`). So a devotee looking at "₹0 of ₹15,000" who tries to
give is turned away with **KMS-400068 / HTTP 409, "this item is unavailable"** — on an item the same
screen says nobody has paid for. Proved by `anAlreadyFulfilledItemStaysClosedToGiving`, which asserts
the 409 and the KMS code after striking the gift. Fixing the three sums does not open that door,
because the door is held by the status guard, not by the arithmetic.

**Does it self-limit? Yes, but on a clock that started before the void.**
`archiveFulfilledForCurrentTenant()` archives FULFILLED items whose `fulfilled_at` is older than the
tenant's window (`wishlist_fulfilled_visible_days`, default 7), and it looks only at the clock — the
progress figure is irrelevant to it. Verified: after backdating `fulfilled_at` ten days the sweep
moved the item to ARCHIVED and it left `forGiving()`. So the contradiction is bounded. But
`fulfilled_at` is **not** reset by a void, so the window is measured from when the item was fulfilled,
not from when the gift was struck: a gift struck on day 6 leaves a contradictory row for one day, and
a gift struck on day 8 lands on an item that is already archived and invisible to donors — the figure
then only moves on the admin list.

**Anything else that now disagrees with itself:**

- **`fulfilled_at`** still records the moment the item was covered, justified by money that no longer
  stands. Nothing reads it except the sweep, but it is a fact the row asserts and cannot support.
- **The "already fully sponsored" notification.** When a gift was diverted to general funds because
  the item was full, the donor was told so (`WISHLIST_SPONSORSHIP_CONVERTED`). Voiding the gift that
  filled the item does not retract that message, and the donor's money stays in general funds. My
  second sponsorship test proves the *capture* path now behaves correctly going forward — a payment
  settling after the covering gift was struck is kept against the item rather than converted — but
  conversions that already happened are not revisited. That is not something a sum can fix.
- I did not open anything under `frontend/` (contract), so I have not checked how a progress bar
  renders `paid_inr = 0` against a FULFILLED badge. The API field is what I measured.

## Deferred to Rajeev: does a fulfilled item reopen?

Not built, not guessed at. `markFulfilledIfComplete` only ever runs `ACTIVE → FULFILLED`, and nothing
anywhere re-evaluates an item afterwards. I added no reopening transition, did not relax the
`status = 'ACTIVE'` guard, added no `AuditAction`, and touched no `wishlist_items.status` transition.

### What the code does today, now the fix has landed

Stated as behaviour. A wish-list item is covered, the temple thanks the donor and marks it fulfilled.
Later an administrator strikes that gift — it was entered twice at the gate, or charged to the wrong
donor, or the bank reversed it.

1. The item **stays FULFILLED**. Nothing looks at it again.
2. Its progress figure **drops to what actually stands** — ₹0, if that was the only gift.
3. It **stays on the giving page** for the rest of the tenant's visibility window (7 days by default,
   counted from when it was fulfilled, not from the void), reading "FULFILLED — ₹0 of ₹15,000".
4. A devotee who tries to give towards it is **refused with KMS-400068**, because checkout refuses on
   status before it looks at money.
5. When the window elapses, the daily sweep **archives it** and it disappears from the giving page.
   It is never bought, never re-offered, and nobody is told.

The net effect of leaving it alone is that a temple which needed a ₹15,000 mixer, was told it had
one, and then found the gift was not real, quietly loses the wish. The item goes off the list without
the mixer ever being bought, and the only trace is a struck row in the donation ledger.

### The options

**A. Leave it FULFILLED — today's behaviour, and the status quo if nothing is decided.**
Nobody is told anything, which is its virtue: no donor who was thanked for completing the item ever
learns that their completion was withdrawn, and no donor is asked twice for something they already
gave. Who is surprised: the **temple**. It believes it owns a grinder it does not own, and finds out
when someone goes to look for it. And the surprise is silent — the item vanishes on schedule, so
there is no moment at which anyone is prompted to notice. The kitchen's wish is lost rather than
refused.

**B. Reopen it to ACTIVE when the money behind it falls short.**
The books then tell the truth: the item is owed money again, appears as ACTIVE, and devotees can give
towards it. Who is surprised: the **donor**. Somebody who was thanked — "the mixer you sponsored is
fully covered" — sees the mixer back on the giving page asking for ₹15,000. If it was *their* gift
that was struck (a chargeback, a duplicate they themselves reported) that is exactly right and they
will expect it. If it was somebody else's gift that was struck, the donor who really did give ₹5,000
towards a jointly funded item sees it reopen and may reasonably think their gift went missing. There
is a second-order cost too: an administrator whose correction is *void-then-re-record* — striking a
gift only to enter it correctly a moment later — would reopen the item and immediately re-fulfil it,
firing whatever the fulfilment flip fires a second time. Building this means a new transition, an
audit action for it, and a decision about `fulfilled_at`; none of that is written.

**C. Leave the status alone but stop letting a fulfilled item hide a shortfall — surface it to
admins.** The item stays FULFILLED and stays off the donor's reach exactly as today, but the temple
gets told: the item appears on an admin list as "fulfilled, but ₹15,000 of its funding was struck",
and a human decides whether to reopen it, buy it anyway, or let it go. Who is surprised: nobody. The
donor's promise is not withdrawn behind their back and the temple does not silently lose a wish. The
cost is that it does nothing on its own — a temple that ignores the flag ends up exactly at option A,
and it is the most work of the three.

**D. Refuse the void while it would un-fund a FULFILLED item**, making the admin un-fulfil the item
deliberately first. This is in the spirit of `DonationVoidService`, which already prefers refusing a
second void loudly over succeeding silently. But it makes an 80G figure — the one number in this
product the temple reports to the tax authority — uncorrectable in order to protect a wish-list row,
and V104's own comment rejects exactly that trade in the donated-equipment case ("the alternative
would leave the 80G figure permanently wrong to protect a register entry an admin can already correct
by hand"). I record it for completeness and would argue against it.

### What a reader needs in order to rule on this

- **How often can this happen?** A void is a rare correction, and a void of a gift large enough to
  have completed a wish-list item is rarer still. This is not a hot path; it is a correctness and
  trust question, not a volume one.
- **Nobody is currently notified either way.** There is no "your sponsorship was withdrawn" message
  in the product, and none of the options above brings one for free. Option B reopens the item
  silently unless a notification is also decided on.
- **The window is short.** Whatever is decided, a contradictory row survives at most the tenant's
  visibility window (default 7 days), because the archive sweep runs on the clock and ignores the
  money. That bounds the damage of doing nothing — but it also means doing nothing quietly destroys
  the wish rather than leaving it visibly broken for someone to notice.
- **My recommendation, since Commandment 9 asks for one rather than a menu:** option **C**, then
  reconsider B once there is a way to tell a donor their sponsorship was withdrawn. B is the honest
  answer to the books and the wrong answer to the person who was already thanked, and the product has
  no vocabulary today for un-thanking somebody. C keeps the donor's promise, stops the temple losing
  a wish silently, and leaves the reopening decision with a human who knows why the gift was struck —
  which is the fact none of the code has.
