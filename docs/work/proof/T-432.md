# T-432 — Inventory, reimagined: three new facts, one unit per row, and a reorder level the server checks

Rajeev, 2026-09-20: the Inventory page *"is very confusing and not up to the standard of other pages
in our app. It needs to be reimagined."*

## What the screen is now

**The list** is one table read left to right as a sentence: **Item · On hand · Available · On order ·
Lasts · Last counted**, and an Edit control. Three columns went and three arrived.

- **Location** became the muted line under the name (`Puja · Puja store`), which is exactly how the
  item's own page has always shown it. One fact in one place, and the row no longer spends a whole
  column on "Main store".
- **Status** went. Its commonest value was the word *Fine*, printed a hundred times down a column a
  reader is scanning for the exceptions. The **Low** and **Expiring soon / Expired** badges now sit
  beside the name they are about — which is also where the card layout puts them below 1024.
- **Committed** went, and this is a deliberate departure from T-086, which put it there on purpose so
  the three figures would add up in front of the reader. It was measured, not argued — see
  *The column that had to go* below. It is still on the item's own page, where it is listed meal by
  meal, which is the only place the number is answerable.
- **On order**, **Lasts** and **Last counted** arrived (Rajeev's items 5, 6 and 7).
- A **search box** was added. 114 consumables and the only way to reach one was to scroll. It matches
  name *or* category, case-insensitive "contains", so "puja" finds the camphor and the agarbatti
  together and "leaf" finds the leaf plates.
- The search box, the shelf filter and the attention chip are **one row**. They were two stacked
  blocks with a blank half-row beside each.

**The item page** keeps its large On hand figure and gains three boxes in the card under it, so the
card is six boxes in two even rows of three (two of two at 390) rather than three with a gap.

**5. Last counted.** An `ADJUSTMENT` carrying `COUNT_CORRECTION`, dated in the temple's own zone.

- **The compensating reversal is excluded.** `StockMovementService.compensate` writes an
  `ADJUSTMENT`/`COUNT_CORRECTION` too, marked `reference_type = 'CORRECTION'`. Nobody counted
  anything; the application put back what a mistaken movement took.
- **Spoilage, damage and waste are not counts.** They are things that happened to the stock.
- **The opening count is included**, deliberately. It is a person standing in the store with scales,
  written through the same code the item page's adjustment form uses. Excluding it would tell every
  temple that has just started tracking that it has never counted anything, on the day it counted
  everything.
- **A count that was later reversed still counts.** The date says when somebody went and looked, and
  somebody did.

On the real temple: **111 of 114** have a date (all 20 Sept 2026 — the seed's opening counts), three
have never been counted and say **"Never counted"** rather than a dash, because a dash in that column
reads as "we failed to fetch it".

**6. On order — and a DRAFT order does not count.**

`pol.quantity − COALESCE(received, 0)`, summed in base units, for `po.status IN ('SENT',
'PARTIALLY_RECEIVED')`.

**The decision, and why.** A draft has not been sent to anybody. Nothing is coming, nobody has
promised anything, and a column saying "200 Kg on order" because a colleague started typing this
morning is a lie that stops a temple buying rice it needs. Two things in the code point the same way:
`VendorPerformanceService:241` holds `LIVE_ORDER = "po.status NOT IN ('DRAFT', 'CANCELLED')"`, and the
donor-facing page once summed purchase-order lines with **no** status filter so drafts counted as
money spent — a defect no test caught, because the fixture helper also left the status to the column
default.

It is deliberately a **different answer** from `ShoppingListService.ingredientsOnLiveOrders()`, which
*does* include DRAFT. That method decides whether to suggest buying something again, and re-suggesting
what a colleague is drafting is a nag. Suppressing a suggestion and stating a fact are different
questions. `RECEIVED` and `CLOSED` are out for a simpler reason: the first has arrived, and what the
second still owes is the shopping list's subject, not something on its way here.

**On the untouched temple, nothing is on order, and that is the truth rather than a bug.** The only
purchase order that is not `RECEIVED` is a single `DRAFT`. To see the column working I flipped that
one order to `SENT`, measured, and **put it back**:

```
Agarbatti PIECES onOrder 24 | onHand 78
Camphor   KG     onOrder 2  | onHand 7
Cotton wick KG   onOrder 3  | onHand 10
Matchbox  PIECES onOrder 60 | onHand 220

-- afterwards --

select status, count(*) from purchase_orders group by 1;            DRAFT|1  RECEIVED|11
select count(*) from inventory_items;                               114
```

**7. How long it lasts — "Approximately", and honest when it cannot tell.**

The rule, written down in `StockFactsService`:

| | |
|---|---|
| Window | the last **90 days** (`HISTORY_DAYS`) |
| Enough history | drawn on **≥ 6 separate days** (`MIN_DAYS_USED`) **and** spanning **≥ 14 days** (`MIN_SPAN_DAYS`) |
| Rate | total drawn ÷ days from the **first draw in the window to today, inclusive** |
| Cover | ⌊on hand ÷ rate⌋, capped at **90 days** (`COVER_CAP_DAYS`) |
| Counts as a draw | `CONSUMPTION` and `ISSUE`, summed through `to_on_hand_qty` |
| Dated by | the meal's own day where the row belongs to a meal, else when it was recorded |

**Days, not movements**, because one meal writes one movement per lot it draws from: a Sunday feast
that emptied three batches of rice is three rows and one day's evidence. **Both bars, not either:**
six days inside one week is a festival, not a rate; a fortnight holding two days is two draws.
Rajeev's own test of the idea was that "an item consumed twice in a month is not a trend", and the
pair refuses that case and everything weaker.

**Against `on hand`, not against `available`.** The rate is measured from what the kitchen has already
drawn, and the meals that make up `committed` are the next part of that same stream — subtracting them
*and* dividing by a rate built from their predecessors would charge the same food twice.

**What the screen will say, and nothing else** (`stockCoverPhrase`):

- **"Not enough history"** — no rate. Never a number, never a hedge, never a blank.
- **"None left"** — nothing on the shelf. "Approximately 0 days" answers a question nobody asked.
- **"Less than a day"** — there is stock and today will finish it.
- **"More than 3 months"** — past the evidence. Ninety days of history divided into a four-year shelf
  is a division, not a forecast.
- **"Approximately 12 days"** otherwise, with his word on every one of them.

**Colour.** Amber only when it runs out inside a week — a week is the ordinary shopping cycle and the
same seven days the temple's expiry warning defaults to. *"Not enough history"* is **never** coloured:
it is the absence of a judgement, and colouring an absence teaches people that amber means "the
computer is unsure".

On the real temple, **47 of 114** clear both bars and 67 do not — which is the shape a real answer
should have. Four of them, read off the live API:

```
Rice           onHand 100     perDay 82.609  → days 1   runsOutOn 2026-09-21
Toor dal       onHand 50      perDay 16.739  → days 2   runsOutOn 2026-09-22
Sambar powder  onHand 4.664   perDay 0.232   → days 20  runsOutOn 2026-10-10
Almond         onHand 1.96    lastsFor null  → "Not enough history"
```

### Two judgements inside item 7

**`ISSUE` counts; `USED_BEYOND_RECORDED_STOCK` does not — and the second went the other way first.**

`ISSUE` is stock sent to a child kitchen (V77). It left this store and will not come back, so as far
as this shelf is concerned it was used. Leaving it out would tell a temple that supplies a satellite
kitchen that its rice lasts far longer than it does.

For the shortfall row, the brief said including it "would double-count or invent consumption". I
checked instead of assuming, and **that reason is wrong**. `InventoryConsumptionService.bookShortfall`
writes the row for *the part of one requirement the batches could not cover*, **in addition to** the
draws that covered the rest — never instead of them. On the seeded temple, 82 (dish, ingredient)
pairs carry both:

```sql
select count(*) from stock_movements a join stock_movements b
  on a.reference_id=b.reference_id and a.ingredient_id=b.ingredient_id
 and a.movement_type='CONSUMPTION' and b.movement_type='USED_BEYOND_RECORDED_STOCK';
-- 82
```

So adding the two would double-count nothing, and leaving the second out under-states how fast a
temple gets through the ingredients it is shortest of.

**I still left it out, and a repo-wide guard is why.** I built it in, ran the wider suite, and
`StockMovementLedgerIT.everyLedgerSumGoesThroughTheOnHandFunction` went red: *"a file that reads
`stock_movements` may not call to_base_qty — it must go through `to_on_hand_qty` (V116), which is
where the exclusion lives, once."* `to_on_hand_qty` returns zero for a shortfall, so counting one
means summing this ledger a second way, and a second way of summing this table is the defect T-122
exists to stop — six readers of one ledger coming to different answers about how much rice there is.
That guard is not in my contract and a slightly conservative rate is the smaller cost, so the rate is
`CONSUMPTION` + `ISSUE`, summed through `to_on_hand_qty`.

It errs towards saying stock lasts **longer** than it will for an ingredient the temple has been
cooking beyond its books. On the data we have those items read "None left" anyway, because a temple
cooking past its books is holding nothing. **Changing it means changing that guard, which is
Rajeev's call.** Two tests cover the decision as it stands:
`usedBeyondTheBooksIsNotCountedTowardsTheRate`, and `aShortfallBesideADrawDoesNotDisturbTheRate`,
which puts a real draw and a shortfall on each of the same six days and asserts the rate is the
draws' 4 Kg a day and not 40.

The same guard also sent the **on-order** query's unit conversion out of SQL and into Java, through
`InventoryUnits.toBase` — a file-level text match cannot tell that that conversion is over
`purchase_order_lines` rather than the ledger. `ShoppingListService.poOutstandingByIngredient` already
does the identical arithmetic the identical way, so the two now match.

**A draw is dated by its meal's day, not by `created_at`.** A meal's draws are written when the meal
is recorded as cooked, which is usually the day it was cooked and sometimes days later. This is not a
nicety on the data we have: every row in the seeded temple's ledger has a `created_at` of the day the
seed ran, while the meals they belong to span 2026-08-29 to 2026-09-18.

```
min/max created_at over 1,532 CONSUMPTION rows : 2026-09-20 / 2026-09-20
min/max plan_date  over the same rows          : 2026-08-29 / 2026-09-18
```

Dating by `created_at` would compress three weeks of cooking into one afternoon and report every
consumable as running out today. **Staging is seeded the same way, so the column would have been
nonsense on the screen Rajeev opens.** `ISSUE` has no planned day and is dated by when it was
recorded, because that *is* when it happened.

## The reported row: one row, one unit

`format.ts` gained **`oneUnitFor(unit, figures)`** — a renderer that picks one unit for a set of
figures and says all of them in it. `quantity()` is **unchanged**: it has about forty callers, its
behaviour is pinned by `quantities.test.ts`, and a figure standing on its own should still be said the
way a person says it. This is a second entry point, the same shape as the `entryQuantity`/`fromEntry`
pair.

**Which unit wins: the biggest figure's.** Reading 1.96, 2.04 and −0.08 Kg is one scale; reading 1,960,
2,040 and −80 gm is the same numbers with three noughts on each.

**Before and after for the exact row he found**, from the live 114-item temple. He reported *2.06 Kg on
hand, 2.04 Kg committed, 20 gm available*; the row is **Almond**, and today's figures differ only
because the buying horizon has moved a day:

```
GET /api/v1/inventory/items → Almond
  onHand 1.96   committed 2.04   available -0.08   (all in KG)

BEFORE — quantity() asked once per figure:
  on hand "1.96 Kg"   committed "2.04 Kg"   available "-80 gm"     ← the scale changes mid-row

AFTER — oneUnitFor(KG, [1.96, -0.08, null]) for the row:
  on hand "1.96 Kg"                         available "-0.08 Kg"
```

Four of the 114 rows changed unit mid-row before this: **Almond**, **Dates** (100 gm / 20.4 Kg /
−20.3 Kg), **Dry coconut** (1.2 Kg / 850 gm / 350 gm) and **Raisin** (2.535 Kg / 255 gm / 2.28 Kg).

**Where it is used, and where it is not.**

| Used | Left on `quantity()` |
|---|---|
| The inventory row: on hand, available, on order | The movement history's **Change** column |
| The item page: the big on-hand figure, all six boxes in the card, the rate behind the estimate | |
| The item page's lot table, its committed-meals table, and the lot dropdown in the adjustment form | |

The movement history is a ledger of separate events, not a set that sums to anything on the page — a
200 Kg delivery and a 5 gm correction of cardamom are both in it, and forcing the second onto the
first's scale would cost more than the alignment is worth. Everything else on the item page is one
scale, including the lots, which have to add up to the header figure.

**A rounding flaw I found in my own helper while driving the app.** The item screen showed a draw of
`-418.2 gm` beside a lot of `4.664 Kg`. Restating 418.2 gm in kilograms is 0.4182, and three decimals
— what a ledger figure gets — would have printed `0.418 Kg` and lost two hundred milligrams out of a
figure somebody reconciles against. `oneUnitFor` now asks each figure how many places it takes to say
it **exactly**, up to six, and keeps the largest. `quantities.test.ts` pins it:
`oneUnitFor("GM", [4664, 418.2])` gives `"4.664 Kg"` and `"0.4182 Kg"`.

**The divergence with printed documents, named rather than left to be discovered.** `Quantities.java:137`
has the identical `>= 1000` rule for the job card, work order and recipe card, and nothing there has
changed. **A printed sheet can therefore still switch unit within a row where the screen no longer
does.** I left it: those documents are generated per line rather than per row, the fix belongs with
whoever owns `document/`, and it is not in this contract. It should be raised as its own item.

## Statement count on the 114-item temple — measured, and it caught a defect I had introduced

Measured with PostgreSQL's own `log_statement='all'` against the live `kms_wave5` database while one
real signed-in `GET /api/v1/inventory/items` was served, then reset.

**My first version sent 145 statements**, of which **114 were one line**:
`SELECT timezone FROM tenants` — `TempleClock.zone()` is a query, and `toItemView` was calling
`LocalDate.now(clock.zone())` once per consumable. That is exactly the N+1
`ShoppingListStatementCountIT` exists to catch, and it was invisible until the log was read.

**After hoisting the clock read out of the loop: 31 statements for 114 consumables.** The full log,
grouped:

**Each of the three new facts is exactly one statement for the whole screen**, whatever the temple

## Things I did not do, and what needs Rajeev

**`ItemCombobox` was not used, and this is the one place I did not do what the brief suggested.**
It is the obvious control for a 114-row picker and I did not reuse it, for two reasons, both of which
would need a new prop — and the contract says stop and report rather than add one:

1. It always offers *"Add ‘&lt;text&gt;’ as a one-off item"* when nothing matches, with no way to
   switch that off. There is no such thing as a one-off inventory item: wiring that control to
   nothing is worse than having no type-ahead.
2. Its second group's visible divider is the hard-coded string **"Other ingredients"**. Whichever way
   round the two groups go, one of them gets that heading — and calling brooms and leaf plates
   "other ingredients" is the exact distinction this task exists to make visible.

What I built instead is a native `<select>` with `<optgroup>` headings (which a browser gives its own
type-ahead), plus the new search box on the list itself. **If Rajeev wants the type-ahead here, the
fix is two props on `ItemCombobox`** — one to suppress the one-off offer and one to name the second
group — and it is a small, separate piece of work.

**The printed documents still switch unit mid-row.** `Quantities.java:137` holds the identical
`>= 1000` rule for the job card, work order and recipe card. The screen now agrees within a row and a
printed sheet does not. I left it: those are generated line by line rather than row by row, and
`document/` is outside this contract. **It should be raised as its own item** rather than found later.

**The shortfall row is out of the run-out rate**, against what the measurement says, because a
repo-wide guard forbids the second way of summing the ledger that counting it would need. Set out in
full above. **It is Rajeev's call**, and it means changing `StockMovementLedgerIT`.

**Dropping the Committed column is a deliberate departure from T-086.** Measured reasons above. If he
