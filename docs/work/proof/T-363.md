## What Rajeev reported, and what was actually wrong

> *"On Monday, 28 September, I created an outside event 'Children's Bhagavad-gita Reading'. Now I cant
> open it to adjust it OR view what is in it, cant print a Job card. NOTHING!!"* … *"THEN why is it
> showing up under September 28'th? That makes absolutly no sense"*

Three complaints, one cause, and it is not what it looks like. **Nothing has ever excluded outside
events from the planner's day list.** I confirmed that in the source rather than taking it from the
brief: `ServedMealService.list()` builds `WHERE 1 = 1` plus the two date bounds and nothing else —
no `is_outside`, no meal-kind, no kitchen predicate — and orders by `pd.plan_date, m.ready_by, …`.
An outside event is an ordinary `meals` row with `is_outside = true` and it sorts among the rest by
when it is due. `grep -c isOutside frontend/components/planner/MealServices.tsx` returned **0**: the
meal card had no outside branch at all, so an event drew exactly like a Lunch.

What was wrong was the separate *Upcoming outside commitments* card at the foot of the planner:

- **It took no date.** `OutsideCommitments` called `api.outsideCommitments(t)` with no argument and
  was rendered outside all three `view ===` branches. The server clause was "everything from today
  onwards". So an event on the 26th sat under a day panel correctly saying the 28th was empty. That
  is the whole of *"why is it showing up under September 28th?"* — it wasn't. The card was never
  about the day.
- **Its rows were dead text.** Every cell was a bare `<td>`; no `<Link>`, no `href`, no Edit, no job
  card — although `c.mealId` was right there as the React key and `OutsideCommitment.java` says the
  id is carried *"so that a row on this list opens that meal"*. That is *"I cant open it to adjust
  it OR view what is in it, cant print a Job card."*

So all three complaints dissolve when the card goes and the event is read in the day list. Asserted,
not assumed — `MealPlanIT.outsideEventsAreOrdinaryMealsInTheDayList` plants a Lunch at 12:00, the
event at 16:00 and a Dinner at 19:30 and requires the event to come back second, with its handover,
contact and address on it, and to open by its own id.

## The two decisions that were mine

**The window: a fortnight, from tomorrow.** Bounded by meaning rather than by a row count — a cap of
"the next five" hides the sixth and the reader cannot know there was one. A fortnight is the span in
which a delivery is still a surprise; six weeks out it is the planner's business and nobody's morning
problem. **From tomorrow, not today**, because today's own outside meals are already on this screen
in the meals card above, and listing one twice would say the temple had two of them. Both asserted:
`TodayIT.todaysOwnOutsideEventIsNotOnTheHeadsUp` and `beyondTheFortnightIsNotAHeadsUp` (day 14 in,
day 15 out).

**Where a row goes: `/planner?date=…`.** Not `/planner/meal/{id}`. The edit form is hidden once a
meal is recorded, so a row into it would start refusing readers the day the meal was written down;
the day view has the meal card with its Edit, its job card and everything else. It is also what
Today's own meal rows already do, so the two lists on one screen behave the same way.

**The pickup label: "They collect it".** Settled with the installed `ux-writing` skill against its
four standards. It mirrors "We deliver it" word for word in shape — pronoun, present-tense verb,
"it" — so a reader who has learned one has learned both, and it names who moves the food, which is
the fact. Rejected: "Collected" (the deleted table's word; past tense on a meal nobody has cooked),
"For collection" (says nothing about who), "They come for it" (vaguer, no shorter).

**Both pills blue, and the two-blues question answered by measurement.** Colour in this product is
severity and never category — `Badge.tsx`: *"Semantic colour is reserved strictly for status … A
badge is never decoration."* A collection nobody turns up for and a delivery nobody drives are the
same failure, so giving them different colours would say one mattered more. The brief asked me to
check a card carrying both an Ekadashi badge and a handover pill. **No card ever carries both**, and
that is measured rather than reasoned: on a planner day with an Ekadashi and two outside events, the
Ekadashi badge is in the day-summary panel (`closest('.card')` → none) at y=241, and the pills are
inside two separate meal cards at y=736 and y=1162. All three are `rgb(229,242,253)` on
`rgb(50,96,134)` — `#E5F2FD` / `#326086`, the info tokens exactly.

## The refusal, and why it is a mechanism and not a coincidence

`WrongRole` gained an optional `reason`. Without one the generic sentence stands, which is all that
is true when the only thing wrong is the role. With one it renders the catalogue's message and next
step, and quotes the code as quietly as `AccountDisabled` quotes `KMS-400019`.

The words are **written in the client**, and that is forced rather than chosen: the guard refuses
before any request is made — deliberately, so a planner page does not start asking for a week it
would be refused — and `/whoami` carries only the `canPlanMeals` boolean. There is nothing on the
wire to read. That is exactly the shape T-116 was written about, so the mechanism goes around it
instead: `refusal-words-reach-the-reader.test.tsx` parses `ErrorCode.java`'s source and asserts this
screen renders `KMS-400183`'s message and next step. **Negative control, and it was shown to apply:**


Reword the sentence in the screen and the test goes red. The same holds the other way: reword
`ErrorCode.java` and it goes red too, which is the direction that actually happens.
