## The design question you asked me to decide: `DELETE` has no body

**I moved it. `POST /api/v1/shifts/{id}/signups/{userId}/release`, and the `DELETE` is withdrawn.**
The reasoning is in the endpoint's javadoc as you asked, and in short:

1. **A `DELETE` with a body** is legal under RFC 9110 but the spec gives it no defined semantics, and
   some proxies and client libraries drop it. A body that vanishes in transit *here* is a removal
   stored with no reason and no note — which is exactly the defect this task closes, reappearing in
   production, behind somebody's corporate proxy, months later.
2. **Query parameters** were never really on the table: the internal note is a coordinator's private
   sentence about a devotee, and query strings are written to every access log on the way.
3. **A POST naming the act** also describes what actually happens better. Nothing is deleted and
   never was — the row keeps its identity, gains `released_at`, a reason and a note, and stays on the
   roster where a coordinator can see what was decided. A thing that records why it happened is an
   event.

**Withdrawn rather than left delegating**, because the `DELETE` has exactly one caller (this repo's
own frontend), the product is pre-beta, and leaving it open would leave a door through which a
volunteer is still removed with no reason and no note. A stale client now gets this project's own
settled answer to the right address with the wrong verb — `KMS-400030`, "what you asked for is not
there" (`GlobalExceptionHandler#handleWrongMethod`); I had written 405 into the javadoc and the test
and the repo corrected me, which is how I found it.

**The permission gate and the separate-endpoint reasoning survive intact.** The endpoint is still
`@PreAuthorize("hasAuthority('MANAGE_VOLUNTEER_SHIFTS')")`, still names the person in the path, and
is still a different endpoint from the volunteer's own `POST /shifts/{id}/release`, which still takes
no "whose spot" parameter at all. `volunteerCannotReleaseSomebodyElse` was updated to press on the
*new* verb, because a permission annotation lost in a rewrite is precisely the kind of thing nobody
notices.

## Which table the columns went on, and why

**`shift_signups`**, as `released_reason` and `released_note`.

A removal is a fact about one person's place on one shift, and that row already carries the other
half of it in `released_at`. Keeping the three together is the reasoning V110 gives for
`attendance_corrected_at`: they cannot drift apart if they cannot be written apart, and the roster
reads them with no join.

A `shift_signup_removals` table was the alternative and is worse. A signup is released at most once
— `shift_signups_one_active` is partial on `released_at IS NULL`, so signing up again after a removal
makes a *new* row — so it would be strictly 1:0..1, joined on every roster read, for two columns.

**Nullable, and that is load-bearing.** A volunteer's own release has no reason to give and is not
asked for one. So the *presence* of a reason is itself how the roster tells the temple's act from the
devotee's, which is a small bonus the pair paid for by itself. Three CHECKs carry what `NOT NULL`
cannot: the two arrive together or not at all; a reason cannot sit on a row nobody released; and the
vocabulary is the four you named and no fifth.

**No backfill**, so nothing here RLS can silently skip. NULL already means what every existing
released row means — nobody recorded a reason, because there was no way to — and inventing one for
old rows would be a record of something nobody said.

## Acceptance criteria

| Criterion | How it was verified |
|---|---|
| **Removing a named volunteer requires both fields** | `bothFieldsAreRequired` — no reason → `KMS-400001` with `fieldErrors[*].field` containing `reason`; no note → the same naming `internalNote`; a note of three spaces → the same again, which is the assertion that says `@NotBlank` and not `@NotEmpty`. It then asserts the volunteer is **still on the shift** after all three refusals, so none of it can pass against a controller that refused the body and removed them anyway. On the client: `will not remove anyone until both halves are given` presses the real button on an empty form and asserts the API was never called. |
| **The volunteer receives the structured reason** | `removedVolunteerIsToldTheReasonAndNeverTheNote` asserts one `REMOVED_FROM_SHIFT` notification exists for them and that its params carry `the rota changed`. Control B proves that assertion is what fails when the message is taken away. |
| **The volunteer never receives the internal note** | The three-way absence check above, plus control A. |
| **The note is on the audit trail** | `removalIsOnTheAuditTrailWithTheNote` — one `VOLUNTEER_REMOVED_FROM_SHIFT` event, `reason` equal to the note exactly, the volunteer named in both states, and `ROTA_CHANGED` in the after-state. Every value in it is read back from the row, not from the request. |
| **The note is on the roster** | `coordinatorReleasesNamedVolunteer` asserts `signups[0].releasedReason` and `signups[0].releasedNote` on the API; `shows the reason and the internal note against someone the temple took off` asserts both render, with the note labelled "not sent to them". |
| **The waitlist promotion message is unchanged** | `waitlistPromotionMessageIsUnchanged` asserts `WAITLIST_PROMOTED`'s parameter key set is *exactly* the five it always was — no reason and no note added — and that the note is not in its params. `NotificationTemplate.WAITLIST_PROMOTED` itself is byte-for-byte untouched. |

Two criteria I added because the pair only means something with them:

- **A volunteer's own release still records nothing** — `ownReleaseCarriesNoReason`, which also
  asserts no removal message was sent to somebody who removed themselves.
- **A reason outside the four is refused** — `unknownReasonIsRefused`. The vocabulary is closed
  because this half is *sent*.

### On "record what was stored, not what was asked for"

`releaseVolunteer` does the `UPDATE … RETURNING id`, then re-`SELECT`s `released_at`,
`released_reason`, `released_note` from that row, and both the audit after-state and the reason the
volunteer is told are built from **that** read. `RETURNING` alone would have been near enough today;
the separate read is there so it stays right if a default, a trigger or a later constraint changes
what landing in this row means.

## The finding Rajeev asked for: does anything else read `released_at` and now need to tell the two acts apart?

**Short answer: nothing in the product today gets this wrong. But there is a defect in this family,
and it is on the volunteer's side rather than in a screen or a report.**

`released_at` is read at 26 places, all inside `org.iskcon.kms.shift` — `grep` finds no reader of
`shift_signups` anywhere else in the backend at all, and `TenantExportService` does not carry the
table. They fall into three groups:

**1. Twenty-two ask only "is this person on the roster right now" (`released_at IS NULL`).** The
capacity count, `myShifts`, `availableShifts` and its `callerState`, the overlapping-shift warning,
the duplicate-signup check, broadcast recipients, reminder scheduling and cancellation, attendance
marking and correction, waitlist promotion. **None of these needs the distinction and none is wrong.**
Removed and withdrawn both mean "not on this roster", which is the only thing they ask, and not one
of them says anything *about* the release.

**2. Two render the release as an event** — `ShiftService.roster()` (`:107`, `:118`) and the roster
screen's Released section. **Both now carry the distinction**, and that is this task.

**3. Two skip on `released_at IS NOT NULL`** — `ShiftReminderService:50`, which sends no reminder to
a released spot, and `JobCardService:689`, which leaves released people off a printed job card. Both
are right either way. The second is also why the internal note cannot reach a printed card: the rows
that carry a note are exactly the rows that are skipped, and the card only ever reads a name, a phone
number and a shift title.

**Nothing aggregates releases, and that is worth saying out loud rather than treating as an
absence.** There is no reliability figure and no hours-contributed figure anywhere yet. V110's
comment says they are "computed on demand from this column and stored nowhere"; the accurate
position today is that they are not computed at all. **That is where this distinction will matter
most when it is built**, and it should be written into whatever story builds it: a coordinator's
removal counted as the volunteer's own drop-out would mark somebody's reliability down for something
the temple did to them. The data to avoid that now exists; the figure that would misread it does not
exist yet, which is the good order for the two to arrive in.

### The defect in this family, named

**The removed volunteer's only account of what happened is the message, and the message is
best-effort.**

- `myShifts` filters `released_at IS NULL AND s.status = 'OPEN' AND s.shift_date >= CURRENT_DATE`,
  so a removed shift simply vanishes from My Shifts.
- There is no past-shifts or history surface for a volunteer at all. `/my-shifts` renders `myShifts`
  and `myWaitlist` and nothing else, and `VolunteerShiftController` exposes no other read.
- `notifyRemoval` → `notifyShift` wraps the whole send in `catch (RuntimeException e) { log.warn(…) }`,
  deliberately, and delivery can still fail per channel after queueing.

So a removal whose message does not land puts the volunteer back in exactly the position T-080
exists to fix: the shift is gone and nobody told them. The temple can see the failure in
`notifications.status`. The person it is about is the only party with nowhere to look.

**This is not hypothetical on staging.** Mailgun's sandbox delivers only to pre-authorised addresses,
which is already recorded as a UAT limitation, and it fails in precisely this shape for most test
volunteers.

**Suggested fix, not built and not in this task's contract:** My Shifts grows a short second list —
shifts you were taken off in the last week, each with its structured reason and nothing else. One
relaxed `WHERE` clause and a list on a screen that already exists, and it turns the message from the
sole channel into a convenience. The reason only, never the note.
