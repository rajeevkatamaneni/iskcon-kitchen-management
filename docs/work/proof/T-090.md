## What was built

**Lead time lives on `vendor_supplies`** (V119), nullable, `CHECK (0..365)`, no DEFAULT. It is a fact
about *this vendor supplying this ingredient*: the same merchant can be next-day on rice and a week
on jaggery he has to fetch.

**Somebody can enter it.** There *is* a screen where supply rows are edited — the Supplies section of
`/vendors/[id]` — so the field goes there beside Last price, with a hint that says to leave it blank
if you don't know. Nothing new had to be invented.

**Unknown is not zero.** A blank box posts an explicit `null`, the column stores `null`, and
`LeadTimes.effectiveDays` falls back to the two-day assumption. Zero is kept as a real and different
answer (cash-and-carry). This is guarded at all four points where it could have collapsed: the form
(`Number("")` is 0 — the box is checked for blank first), the request record, `getObject` rather than
`getInt` in both row mappers, and a boxed `Integer` parameter on `effectiveDays` so unboxing at a
call site is a compile error rather than a silent zero.

**The escalation.** `SufficiencyService` gives a **short** meal an `orderBy` date and an
`orderUrgency`; the shopping list gives every computed line the same pair. The words:

| State | Planner badge | Shopping list | Tone |
|---|---|---|---|
| `IN_TIME` | `Short · order by 12 Sep` | `Order by 12 Sep 2026` | warning (amber) |
| `ORDER_TODAY` | `Short · order today` | `Order today` | danger |
| `TOO_LATE` | `Short · won't arrive in time` | `Won't arrive in time` | danger |

**The third state keeps the same red on purpose and the words carry it.** A meal tomorrow that is
short of rice is not less serious because the deadline passed, so escalating the colour a third time
would be wrong in the other direction — and a shade cannot tell a cook which of two quite different
problems they have (get the order out today, or find another way to feed people). The tests assert
the sentences, not the colours, and the negative control patches the third sentence into the second
to prove the distinction is load-bearing.

**Nothing refuses anything.** No new validation gate, no blocked save, no disabled button. The only
new refusal in the whole task is a lead time above 365 or below 0 on the vendor form, which is a
typo guard on a number nobody means.

## The decision the brief asked me to make and write down: the two "2"s are different numbers

`ShoppingListService.LEAD_BUFFER_DAYS = 2` and `frontend/lib/format.ts LEAD_BUFFER_DAYS = 2` are
**not** a lead time, despite the name and despite `leadTimeWarning`'s wording. Reading what the code
does with them:

- `ShoppingListService` writes `needed_by = earliest demand − 2`, and
  `PurchaseOrderService.generate` (`:153`, `:183`, `:190`) copies the minimum of that onto the
  purchase order as **the date the vendor is asked to deliver by**. So it is a **delivery buffer** —
  *get it here two days before we cook* — answering "what date do we write on the order?"
- A **lead time** answers a different question: "when is the last day we can ask?" That answer is the
  order-by date, and nothing in the product could compute it.

**So: a recorded lead time supersedes the assumption *for the order-by date*, and leaves the delivery
buffer alone.** The assumption stays as the fallback where no lead time is recorded, because
inventing a second, different guess so the first could be superseded would leave two numbers arguing
where there is one question. Written down in three places so it cannot drift: the V119 header,
`LeadTimes`' class javadoc, and a new javadoc on `LEAD_BUFFER_DAYS` itself.

**`shopping_list_lines.needed_by` is untouched** and `order_by` is a new column beside it. Changing
`needed_by` would change what every generated purchase order asks a supplier for, which is a decision
about the product rather than a side effect of adding a field.

`frontend/lib/format.ts` and `frontend/app/orders/[id]/page.tsx` are **unmodified** — confirmed by
`git diff --stat`, which returns nothing for either.

## A defect this uncovered and did not fix — for Rajeev

**What the app does today.** When the shopping list is turned into purchase orders, the delivery date
it puts on the order is *two days before the meal*. The order screen then warns about that very date:
*"Sooner than the 2 days a vendor usually gets."*

**Who it affects.** Anyone with Kitchen Staff, Kitchen Manager or Temple Admin — everyone who
generates orders from the shopping list.

**So in plain terms:** the shopping list suggests a delivery date, and the order screen immediately
complains that the date the shopping list chose gives the vendor too little notice.

**A concrete case.** A meal on 12 September, shopping list regenerated on 10 September. The line's
`needed_by` becomes 10 September, the purchase order carries 10 September, and the order screen shows
the warning because 10 September is today. The two days have been counted twice: once to pull the
delivery date earlier, and again as the notice the vendor is owed.

**Why I did not fix it.** The fix is to stop subtracting the buffer from `needed_by` — that is,
to ask the vendor to deliver *on the day the food is needed* and let the new order-by date carry the
notice. That changes what every generated purchase order asks a supplier for, and
`backend/.../purchaseorder/**` is forbidden ground for me tonight (T-124 and T-126 landed there).

**The choice:** leave the delivery buffer where it is and accept that the order screen warns about
dates the app itself chose, or drop it now that order-by exists to do the job it was doing badly.
I recommend dropping it, as its own task.

## One presentational change I made deliberately, and would expect him to look at

**The shopping list's "Needed by" column is now "Order by".** With no lead time recorded the two
dates are *identical* (demand − 2 both ways), so keeping both would have put two columns side by side
showing the same date under two names. The delivery date is still in the payload and still on every
purchase order and its own screen; what a person reading a shopping list wants to know is when it has
to go out, and until now nothing on that screen answered it. Easy to revert — it is presentational.

Also on that screen: a muted **"assumed"** appears beside the badge where no lead time is recorded, so
somebody can tell our guess from their vendor's word. It is the nudge towards recording the real one.
