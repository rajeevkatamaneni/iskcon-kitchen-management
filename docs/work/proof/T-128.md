are in different languages and cannot be merged. `RecipeScaler` no longer picks a unit; it asks
`Quantities`, which is where the rule was lifted to in the first place.

## Which of the two the brief offered, and why

**The merge, not a third copy of the patch.** The brief said to check what it would cost first, so:

`RecipeScaler` cannot call `Quantities.cooks` or `Quantities.exact`. Those return a finished string;
`RecipeScaler` returns a `ScaledQuantity` record whose `displayQuantity` and `displayUnit` are
separate fields, because the recipe page puts the number in a column beside the raw one
(`frontend/app/recipes/[id]/page.tsx:360` renders `${displayQuantity} ${displayUnit}` straight from
the API, with no formatter in between). So a whole-string merge was never available.

But the string is not what was duplicated. **Only the unit choice was**, and that half merges
cleanly. It is now `Quantities.displayUnit(unit, inBase)` — public, one method, called by
`Quantities.render` and by `RecipeScaler.scale`, and `RecipeScaler.pickDisplayUnit` is deleted.

**The rounding does not merge, and I did not force it.** `RecipeScaler.round` is
`setScale(2, HALF_UP).stripTrailingZeros()`; `Quantities.roundAsAPersonWould` snaps to a step a
person can weigh to — 135 gm, 10 Kg, 175 L. Those are different jobs for different readers, and
folding the scale preview into the cook's rounding would change every figure on that screen, which
is a product decision and not a duplication. Both classes now say so in their javadoc.

Two consequences worth naming, since a reader in three months is the audience:

- **The `>= 1000` constant and the family pairing now exist once on this side.** `RecipeScaler` used
  to declare the pairing a second time in a `switch (Unit.Family)`; `Quantities.FAMILY` is the only
  declaration now.
- **That switch was exhaustive on purpose** — its comment said a new `Unit.Family` should fail to
  compile there rather than fall through to a wrong unit. `Quantities.FAMILY` is a map, so a new
  family missing from it is shown in its own unit instead of failing the build. That is a real, small
  loss of a compile-time guard, traded for the pairing existing once. It is fail-soft rather than
  fail-wrong, it is already the behaviour for `PIECES`, and I have written it into the `FAMILY`
  field's comment so the next person adding a family sees it. Flagging rather than hiding it.

**The signum() point from the brief is satisfied by construction**, because the zero test moved
wholesale rather than being retyped: `inBase.signum() == 0` is now inside `Quantities.displayUnit`
and both callers reach it. `QuantitiesTest.zeroAtAnyScale` and the `0.000` / `0.00000` vectors in the
new table cover the JDBC-scale case, and `equals(ZERO)` would fail every one of them.

## What stops them drifting again

`QuantitiesTest.UnitChoice` — **one table of seventeen vectors, run through both callers**, which is
the pattern T-127 set. `quantitiesAgrees` asserts `Quantities.exact(...)` ends in the table's unit;
`recipeScalerAgrees` asserts `RecipeScaler.scale(..., ratio 1).displayUnit()` equals the same one.
Re-inlining the rule into `RecipeScaler`, or fixing one caller and not the other, fails here.

The vectors are the ledger form's, not the cook's, deliberately: the cook's form can promote a
*second* time after rounding — 999 gm rounds to 1000 gm, which is a kilo and says so — so its final
unit is legitimately not always the chosen one. That carry stays covered by
`Cooks.roundingCanPromote`, and the table says why it is excluded.

`RecipeScalerTest` gained a javadoc paragraph and nothing else, pointing a reader at that table, so
nobody adds a unit-promotion case back into the file whose self-agreement is what went wrong.

## The negative control — `docs/work/proof/control-T-128.log`

**What it controls, since the fix was a merge rather than an added line:** it puts the *third copy of
the rule back* — `pickDisplayUnit`, exactly as it read before this task, with no zero case — and
leaves `Quantities` alone. That is the live defect reproduced on demand, and it isolates the copy
this task existed to remove.

   The third is `Quantities`' own `if (inBase.signum() == 0) {`, asserted present-and-single so that
   the control is proved to be isolating `RecipeScaler` and not quietly stripping both copies —

### The baseline, and why it is not `HEAD`

T-127's proof explains the trap and this task has the identical shape: **the fix is not in `HEAD`.**
Patching it out restores what `HEAD` already holds, so `git diff HEAD` falls *silent* exactly when
the control is working and *speaks* when it has failed to apply. The signal is inverted, not absent.
