## The fix, and why

**F2.** The cause was in the fitter. Name columns use `overflow-wrap: anywhere`, which makes a
column's minimum width a single letter. When a table was short of room, the short columns kept
their headings on one line whatever it cost, so the Item column took all of the shortfall, down
to 46px. The conductor ruled (2026-09-19, binding): no cards at 1024px or wider; a long heading
such as "Rejected on delivery" may wrap to two lines, and it wraps before any text column gives
up width; Item stays at least as wide as its longest word and is never narrower than any other
text column. Here is how that was built, in order:

1. **Short-column headings wrap first, on two lines.** The fitter measures where each word of the heading falls and works out the narrowest two-line width. The heading that saves the most width wraps first. Cells never wrap. Any width left over is shared evenly between the gaps, as before.
2. **Then text columns give way**, as before. The difference is that the name column's minimum is now its longest whole word, measured with normal wrapping through a new CSS rule in the measuring state. Squeezed columns are held to a common width, so Item is never narrower than another squeezed text column.
3. **Last resort (my judgement, please confirm):** if Item still cannot get its longest word, the headings wrap one word to a line. Measured on the PO page at 1024, two-line headings left the table **1px short** (104px left for Item, whose longest word "Cardamom" needs 105). The only other answer is handing the table back to the browser, which gave Item 49px. A heading three lines tall seemed the smaller harm. It is used only when two lines are not enough.
4. **If nothing fits, the fitter returns null (no change from before).** The browser then lays the table out itself.

**The width I chose as "readable":** the column's longest whole word. That is the conductor's
rule, and it is the point at which no word is broken. On the PO fixture it is 105px.

DESIGN_SYSTEM.md §5 is unchanged. The fix relies on rule 2 ("Text wraps onto a second line only
when the table genuinely lacks room"), as the conductor ruled.

**F3.** Two things in AddLine were wider than the card:
- The "Add an ingredient" select is as wide as its longest option, "Sri Lakshmi cold-pressed groundnut oil".
- The "An item not in the catalogue" box is a fixed 256px.

The fix caps each field at the width of its row (`[&>*]:max-w-full`) and each control at the
width of its field (`max-w-full`). It also makes the grid `grid-cols-1` (`minmax(0,1fr)`).
Without that, the grid column grows to fit the select and the caps have no effect; I measured
this, and the first attempt without it changed nothing. At 1280 none of the caps is reached.

## Acceptance criteria

- **Item column never below a readable width at 1024/1060/1100/1280:** met on the PO page. Item is 122/140/180/360px, and the floor is its longest word (105px). Measured above.
- **Nothing truncated, no horizontal scroll** on the PO page at all four widths: measured above.
- **PO editor 0px overflow at 390:** met. scrollWidth equals clientWidth, and no child extends past the card, on both screens. At 1280 nothing moved: measured above.
- **No other measured table worse:** met. See the before → after list above.
- **A test in table-rule.test.ts pins the new fitter behaviour, and the negative control fails without the fix:** met. 5 of the 6 new tests fail with the fix removed.
