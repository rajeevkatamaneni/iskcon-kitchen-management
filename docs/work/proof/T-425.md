# T-425 — A countable thing is whole wherever the application produces a number

The rounding lives in **one line**, in `recipe/RecipeScaler.java`, at the moment a scaled quantity is
produced. Every consumer reads `ScaledLine.rawQuantity` from that one place, so the recipe scale
preview, the job card, the cooking draw, the planner's sufficiency badge and the cost estimate all
changed together and cannot disagree. **No other service needed touching. No migration, no new error
code, no new permission, no frontend file.**

The money moves with the quantity, measured on a real costing path: a day's estimate of **₹222.00
becomes ₹250.00**, and **cost per serving ₹5.55 becomes ₹6.25**.

## Where the rounding lives, and what in the code settled it

```java
if (unit.family() == Unit.Family.COUNT) {
    BigDecimal whole = raw.setScale(0, RoundingMode.CEILING);
    return new ScaledQuantity(whole, unit.name(), whole, unit.label(whole));
}
```

It was `BigDecimal shown = round(raw); return new ScaledQuantity(raw, …, shown, …)` — the raw
fraction to every consumer, the rounded figure to the screen.

**What settled "here and nowhere else" was reading the call graph, not the brief.** `RecipeScaler.scale`
is called from exactly one place:

```
main/java/org/iskcon/kms/recipe/RecipeService.java:203:  ScaledQuantity q = RecipeScaler.scale(line.quantity(), Unit.valueOf(line.unit()), ratio);
```

and `RecipeService.scale` / `scaleAll` is called from exactly six:

```
costing/BasketCostingService.java:67       ScaledRecipeView scaled = recipeService.scale(recipeId, targetYield);
document/JobCardService.java:685           ScaledRecipeView scaled = recipeService.scale(dish.recipeId(), dish.targetYield());
document/DocumentGenerationService.java:441 ScaledRecipeView scaled = recipeService.scale(recipeId, targetYield);
inventory/InventoryConsumptionService.java:278 ScaledRecipeView scaled = recipeService.scale(recipeId, targetYield);
inventory/CommittedStockService.java:279   List<ScaledRecipeView> views = recipeService.scaleAll(…)
recipe/RecipeController.java:118           return recipeService.scale(id, targetYield);
```

and every one of them reads `rawQuantity`, never `displayQuantity`:

```
costing/BasketCostingService.java:69       basket.add(line.ingredientId(), line.rawQuantity(), Unit.valueOf(line.rawUnit()));
document/JobCardService.java:1030,1034     new MergedLine(…, line.rawQuantity(), …) / existing.quantity().add(line.rawQuantity())
document/DocumentGenerationService.java:451 Quantities.cooks(lines.get(i).rawQuantity(), lines.get(i).rawUnit())
inventory/InventoryConsumptionService.java:293 requiredBase.merge(line.ingredientId(), InventoryUnits.toBase(line.rawQuantity(), unit), …)
inventory/CommittedStockService.java:255   InventoryUnits.toBase(line.rawQuantity(), Unit.valueOf(line.rawUnit()))
```

`SufficiencyService` has no scaling of its own — it reads `CommittedStockService.claimsInHorizon()`,
so it is covered by the same line. So making `rawQuantity` the whole number is the *only* change that
makes all of them agree; rounding in each consumer would have been six edits and three different
answers (see the judgement below).

**CEILING, not HALF_UP.** You cannot cook with 0.78 of a banana. Rounding to nearest is worse than
doing nothing in the small case: a scaled 0.4 coconut reached
`DocumentGenerationService:451 → Quantities.cooks(0.4, PIECES) → setScale(0, HALF_UP)` and the
recipe card in the cook's hand read **"0 pieces"**. CEILING is also what `shoppinglist/BuyingAmount.java:229`
already does for a counted line (`need.setScale(0, RoundingMode.CEILING)`), so what the temple is told
to buy and what the kitchen is told to take now round the same way.

**The two fields collapse for a count, and I think they should.** The record still carries both — a
mass genuinely needs them, 24,000 gm shown as 24 Kg — but for a `COUNT` unit they are one number by
construction, and `ScaledQuantity`'s javadoc now says so. I did not delete the fields: doing that would
change the wire shape of `ScaledLine` and touch the frontend, which is outside the contract, and it
buys nothing the javadoc does not.

## The judgement calls, written down

### Three dishes at 0.4 of a coconut each: three coconuts, not two

Rounded **per line**, inside the scaler, so three dishes are three separate `scale` calls and three
coconuts.

The reason is not arithmetic, it is that **`consume` cannot see the other dishes**.
`InventoryConsumptionService.computePlan` is called with one `recipeId` and one `targetYield`; the
three dishes are three transactions, possibly on three days, in three kitchens. Aggregating across
them would mean the planner's committed column (`CommittedStockService`, which *does* sum across
dishes) said two while the ledger took three — a permanent, silent disagreement between the inventory
screen and `stock_movements`, which is the same class of defect this task exists to remove. The
storekeeper would find the shelf wrong and nothing on any screen would explain it.

### Per line, not per ingredient — and this one cost me an argument against myself

`Quantities`' own javadoc warns: *"Round then compute and the errors stack; compute then round and
they cannot."* Rounding per line does exactly the thing it warns about — a recipe naming coconut on
two lines at 0.4 each draws 2 where the merged 0.8 would draw 1.

I chose per line anyway, and the deciding fact is that **the three consumers merge on three different
keys**:

- `JobCardService.merge` (line 1027): `ingredientId + "|" + rawUnit + "|" + preparationNote` — two
  differently-prepared lines deliberately stay two lines (R-DUP-1).
- `InventoryConsumptionService.computePlan` (line 293) and `IngredientBasket.add`: `ingredientId` alone.

So "round after the merge" gives the printed job card 1 + 1 = 2 and the ledger 1, for the same dish, on
the same day. Rounding per line gives both 2. Self-consistency beats theoretical thrift.

**Measured before choosing, not assumed.** Across the 458 ingredient lines in the three recipe books
this product ships (`recipe-library/karnataka.json`, `andhra_pradesh.json`, `prep-book/curated.json`):

```
ingredient lines: 458 | counted-unit lines: 34 | fractional counted: 0
same ingredient on >1 line (any unit): 0
same COUNTED ingredient on >1 line: 0
```

Unit spread across the same 458 lines, for completeness:

```
'gm' 281   'Kg' 92   'L' 33   'Pieces' 29   'ml' 14   '' 4   'Nos' 3   'pieces' 1   'nos' 1
```

(T-423 reported 35 counted lines to my 34; it parsed the quantity *string* and I read the `qty_unit`
field, and four lines carry no unit at all. The difference is one line and changes nothing — every
counted quantity in the books is whole on either count.)

No shipped recipe names the same ingredient twice at all, so nothing the temple actually cooks pays
for the choice. **Rajeev may want to overturn this one** — it is a one-line change to move the
rounding behind the merge if he decides a coconut split across two preparations should be one coconut.

### What I deliberately did not round

- **`SufficiencyService`'s shortfall figures.** `required` is now whole (it comes from the scaler), but
  `available` is a *stored* balance — possibly 16.78 — and `shortBy = required − available`. Rounding
  `shortBy` up while leaving `available` alone would make the three columns stop adding up on the one
  screen whose job is that they do. Its own comment already says the figures are exact because "the
  ordering pipeline buys against them", and `BuyingAmount` ceilings before anything is bought.
- **`Quantities.cooks`.** Display of a stored row. Untouched, per acceptance criterion 6.
- **The FEFO per-batch draw.** A whole requirement of 3 split across a lot of 2.5 posts a 2.5 movement.
  That is finding 1 of the brief and there is a test for it.
- **`ingredientrequest/`.** Checked rather than assumed: `grep -n "multiply\|divide\|setScale\|RoundingMode\|suggest" *.java`
  in that package returns exactly one hit — `IngredientIssueService.java:147`, the FEFO split above.
  Nothing there derives a quantity from a recipe; every figure is typed and already gated by T-423.
- **The shopping list.** Verified and left alone: `BuyingAmount.java:229` already ceilings a COUNT
  family, and `BuyingAmountTest` still pins `2.1 PIECES → 3`, `0.2 PIECES → 1` and "never rounds down"
  (all three PASSED in the run below).

## What happened to the money — measured, both ways

Fixture: Coconut Rice, 3 coconuts + 10 Kg rice per 100 Kg, planned at 40 Kg for 40 people. Coconut
₹35, rice ₹45, both from the temple's preferred vendor. So the coconut requirement is 1.2 → 2.

Measured by running the same two assertions against the current tree and then against the
pre-T-425 scaler, with the expectation set to a value that cannot match so the real figure prints:

```
=== AFTER ===
    java.lang.AssertionError: JSON path "$.estimatedTotal" expected:<-1.0> but was:<250.0>
    java.lang.AssertionError: JSON path "$.kinds[0].costPerServing" expected:<-1.0> but was:<6.25>
=== BEFORE ===
    java.lang.AssertionError: JSON path "$.estimatedTotal" expected:<-1.0> but was:<222.0>
    java.lang.AssertionError: JSON path "$.kinds[0].costPerServing" expected:<-1.0> but was:<5.55>
PROBE2: restored, cmp clean
```

| | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| `/api/v1/materials-cost` estimatedTotal | ₹222.00 | ₹250.00 |
| `/api/v1/materials-cost/by-meal-kind` costPerServing | ₹5.55 | ₹6.25 |

By hand: 1.2 × 35 + 4 × 45 = 222; 2 × 35 + 4 × 45 = 250. **+₹28 on the day, +₹0.70 a plate, 12.6%.**
The rise is the point: the estimate is now the cost of the two coconuts the kitchen is actually told to
take. A cost worked out from a quantity nobody is allowed to draw is the worse of the two answers, and
that is what `estimatedTotal` was. Both figures are pinned in `WholeCountedRequirementIT`.

**Worth Rajeev knowing:** on a menu with many counted ingredients at small scales this makes the daily
estimate consistently a little higher. It errs towards overstating the bill, which is the direction
`BasketCostingService`'s own javadoc already argues for.

## Acceptance criteria

**1. 0.25 coconut per head × 800 heads = 200 coconuts. Not 800.**
`RecipeScalerTest.scaleFirstThenRoundOnce` (`ratio(1, 800)` applied to a `0.25 PIECES` line → raw 200,
display 200) and `WholeCountedRequirementIT.scaleFirstAndRoundOnce` through
`GET /api/v1/recipes/{id}/scaled?targetYield=800`. Both PASSED. Proved to bite by control case
`roundfirst`, where both fail with `expected:<200> but was:<800>`.

**2. A scaled COUNT requirement is whole and rounded up everywhere it is produced.**
One rounding, at the single production point, so all five consumers get the same figure.
`WholeCountedRequirementIT.everyScreenSaysTheSameWholeNumber` asserts all of them on one 1.2-coconut
requirement: the scale preview (`rawQuantity` 2 **and** `displayQuantity` 2), the printed job card
(HTML contains `2 pieces`, does not contain `1.2 pieces`), the planner's sufficiency badge
(`shortfalls[Coconut].required` = 2), the cost (₹250.00), and the draw (`lines[Coconut].required` = 2,
ledger row `-2`, on hand 10 → 8). Ingredient requests are the one item on the brief's list that needed
nothing: `grep -n "multiply\|divide\|setScale\|RoundingMode\|suggest"` over `ingredientrequest/*.java`
returns one hit, the FEFO split, and every request figure is typed and gated by T-423.

**3. A fractional KG/L/GM/ML requirement is still fractional.**
`RecipeScalerTest.theRuleDoesNotLeakIntoMassOrVolume` — 2.4 Kg, 0.4 Kg, 0.75 L, 12.5 ml — and
`WholeCountedRequirementIT.aMeasuredRequirementIsStillFractional` — 2.4 Kg rice, 0.4 L milk (shown as
400 ml, so the promotion still works too), 250 gm jaggery. **Seven vectors, and the count is the
point:** this asserts an absence, so a vector that was already whole would prove nothing. Every one of
the seven is fractional in its own unit after scaling, so every one of them would move if the branch
stopped testing `Unit.Family.COUNT`. Both convertible families, both directions of promotion, and one
cross-unit case (grammes against an ingredient held in Kg).

**4. The money agrees with the quantity, on a real costing path with a real figure.**
`/api/v1/materials-cost` ₹222.00 → **₹250.00**; `/api/v1/materials-cost/by-meal-kind` costPerServing
₹5.55 → **₹6.25**. Both measured by running the same request against the pre-T-425 scaler and the
current one (output pasted above), and both now pinned in the IT.

**5. Existing fractional rows still read back, still sum, and are still drawable.**
`WholeCountedRequirementIT.theFractionalRowsAlreadyWrittenStillWork`: a lot of 2.5 coconuts written
exactly as staging holds one. On hand sums to **12.5**; `/api/v1/inventory/movements` returns the
2.5 row naming Coconut; a whole requirement of 3 is met FEFO from the 2.5 lot and the next, posting a
**−2.5** CONSUMPTION movement and leaving **9.5**. That is finding 1's case, and it is why the
rounding is at the requirement and not at the draw.
