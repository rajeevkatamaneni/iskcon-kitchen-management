**How the colours were chosen.** I followed the better-colors skill and did the maths in OKLCH, solving each pack with a script. All contrast numbers below are WCAG 2.1 values, computed with the same formula as theme-contract.test.ts.
- The tint (`festival-bg`) sits at hue 84–85, which is saffron the spice (the named colour #F4C430 is hue 89). It uses the most chroma sRGB allows at the lightness where ink-muted still reaches 4.5:1. Within that limit, the hue and lightness were picked to be as far as possible from accent-bg, warning-bg, danger-bg, info-bg, success-bg, canvas and sunken. I tried pale orange tints (hue 55–72) first. They came out almost identical to warning-bg's peach, and in terracotta to accent-bg as well (OKLab distance 0.007).
- The dot (`festival`) and the text (`festival-text`) sit at hue 55, deep saffron. The dot is the lightest value that still reaches 3:1 on canvas, raised, sunken and the tint. The text is the lightest that reaches 4.6:1 on the same four. Chroma depends on the family: bright 0.17/0.15, calm 0.15/0.12, soft 0.11/0.085. So the tint is yellower than the dot and text, which is the same hue drift Radix uses in its orange scale.

**Design system amendment (to fold in)**
> **Festival (saffron).** Festivals and feasts on the Vaishnava calendar and the planner wear their own colour, saffron. It is not a status colour, because green means only that the person's own action succeeded, amber only a warning, and red only something serious (Rajeev, 2026-09-18). It is not the accent either, which on the calendar means a fasting day. Three tokens, in every pack: `festival-bg` is the tint behind a festival day's cell or chip, a pale saffron-yellow; `festival` is the solid dot beside the name and the event's left rule, a deep saffron-orange; `festival-text` is the festival's name set as text, on the tint or on the page. Floors: ink, ink-secondary and ink-muted at 4.5:1 on `festival-bg`; `festival-text` at 4.5:1 on `festival-bg`, canvas, raised and sunken; `festival` at 3:1 on `festival-bg`, canvas, raised and sunken. The Badge component has a `festival` tone. Calendar legend: blue for Ekadashi, accent for a fasting day, saffron for a festival or feast, muted grey for an observance.
