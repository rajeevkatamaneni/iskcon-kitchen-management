## 4 · What is genuinely left, and the design for it, so nobody re-derives this

One thing, and it is real: **`vendor_supplies.preferred` becoming primary-and-secondary.** That
covers ingredients and supplies — everything except equipment. Here is the design, decided rather
than assumed, for whoever gets the re-scoped row.

**Shape: a small integer `preference_rank`, not two booleans, and not a second `secondary` boolean.**

- Two booleans express nonsense the database then has to forbid separately — `preferred AND
  secondary` on one row is a vendor that is its own fallback, and that needs a third constraint on
  top of the two partial indexes.
- One integer is constrained once. `CHECK (preference_rank IS NULL OR preference_rank IN (1, 2))`
  plus `CREATE UNIQUE INDEX ON vendor_supplies (tenant_id, ingredient_id, preference_rank) WHERE
  preference_rank IS NOT NULL` gives exactly the guarantee the brief asks for — **at most one
  primary and at most one secondary per ingredient per temple, in the database** — and the
  "same vendor twice" case is impossible by construction, because one row cannot hold two ranks and
  `vendor_supplies_vendor_ingredient` already makes the pair unique. No new error code is needed for
  it; the trap the brief names cannot be expressed.
- `NULL` means "not a designated source", which is what every unpreferred row means today.
- Migration: add the column, backfill `preference_rank = 1 WHERE preferred` **per tenant** (this one
  *is* DML and *is* subject to RLS, unlike V99 and V119 which were pure DDL — it must adopt each
  tenant in turn, per `V97`/`V98`), then drop `preferred` and its index. `LeadTimes`,
  `BasketCostingService:161` and `VendorService.preferredVendorId` all read `WHERE preferred` and
  all three become `WHERE preference_rank = 1`.

**`VendorService.setSupply` needs the same move it already does for `preferred`**, once per rank:
setting rank 1 clears whoever holds rank 1, setting rank 2 clears whoever holds rank 2, and setting
a rank on a vendor that holds the other rank for that ingredient must clear the other one first or
the row simply changes rank. All in the one transaction, as today.

**The question I could not answer, and it is the third one the brief asked for — what reads the
secondary.** Nothing does, and I could not invent it. `preferredVendorId` returns an `Optional<UUID>`
with exactly one caller shape: "the vendor this line goes to". A secondary is a *fallback*, and
nothing in the ledger or in Rajeev's ruling says **when** the fallback fires. The plausible triggers
are different features:

1. **When the primary is deactivated.** `VendorService.setActive` drops a vendor with a reason, and
   `VendorService.contractEndingSoon`'s javadoc says out loud that a vendor past its contract end
   *"stays the preferred source for whatever they supply, until a person decides otherwise"*. So
   today, dropping a vendor silently leaves every one of its ingredients pointing at a dead supplier.
   A secondary that took over here would be worth having and is a real defect fix.
2. **When the primary has no price**, for `BasketCostingService`, which currently falls back to the
   *dearest* price on record.
3. **When the primary's lead time is too long to make the order-by date**, for T-090's escalation.
4. **Never automatically — it is just written down for a human to ring.**

These are four different products. Guessing between them is what Commandment 9 says not to do.
