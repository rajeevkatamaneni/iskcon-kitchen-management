# T-430 — an ingredient the temple never buys never reads "Low"

`V153` let a temple mark an ingredient as one it never buys, and the shopping list honoured it. The
low-stock judgement did not. On the seeded temple that put **water at minus 1,358.5 litres** in the
low-stock list, in the dashboard's below-par count, in the nightly digest and on the inventory
screen's own badge and tally.

**The product argument, in my own words.** "Low" is an instruction to act, and the action is to buy.
A temple cannot buy water. Minus 1,358.5 litres is not a shortage — it is a ledger artefact of
cooking with something nobody stocks, and it grows for as long as the temple keeps cooking, so it
never clears and never stops shouting. A badge that says act-now when there is nothing anybody can
do breaks the colour rule twice over: amber warns, red means act now, and neither is a true thing to
say about a tap. The real cost is not the wrong badge, it is the real shortage standing behind it —
a digest that opens with 1,358 litres of water is a digest a cook learns to delete unread.

**What changed is only the judgement.** Water is still tracked, still committed, still drawn down,
still costed, and still has its stock detail page, with its true minus-1,358.5 figure shown. Nothing
was filtered out of any list.

## Decisions I was asked to take deliberately

**`itemsTracked` stays as `stock.size()` — water is still counted in the denominator.** The two
numbers answer different questions. `itemsBelowThreshold` asks "how many things should somebody act
on", and a never-bought ingredient is not one of them. `itemsTracked` asks "is this temple tracking
anything at all" — the distinction `TodayView` exists to draw between "0 below par" and "nothing
tracked". Water genuinely is tracked: it has an inventory row, it draws down through the allocator,
it is costed and it has a detail page. Taking it out of the denominator would make that number the
count of *neither* thing. So the seeded temple reads 72 of 114, not 72 of 113. Asserted both ways in
`theDashboardCountDropsItButTheTrackedCountKeepsIt`.

**The second overload, `lowStock(Map)`, was made consistent.** Both call sites now pass the flag.
The reason is the class's own stated invariant, which I would otherwise have silently falsified —
`lowStock(Map)`'s javadoc promises that "the *Low* rule itself is not restated here — both methods
ask `isBelowThreshold`, so the badge on the screen and the line on the shopping list cannot come to
disagree about what low means." Fixing one and not the other would have made that sentence untrue
while leaving it on the page, which is worse than either behaviour on its own.

It changes nothing about the shopping list's output, and that is the point rather than a hope:
`ShoppingListService`'s merge loop drops a marked ingredient by name at `:485-487`, so a threshold
contribution that no longer arrives would have been dropped a few lines later anyway. The list's
single drop-point stays exactly where its comment says it is. `NotBoughtShoppingListIT` passes
unchanged, all six tests, which is the check on that reasoning.

**Reorder suggestions were left alone**, as briefed — I confirmed the merge loop's `if
(ref.notBought()) continue;` at `ShoppingListService.java:485-487` and did not touch that file.
