# T-088 — the planner badge is a property of the meal, not of the range the screen asked about

The brief's correction was right and its diagnosis held up in the code: `SufficiencyService.evaluate`
was already a running allocation, and the defect was that it allocated **only across the range it was
asked about**. `DayView` asks `api.mealSufficiency(date, date)`, so Thursday opened on its own saw
the whole sack.

**The screen is unchanged.** The fix is that the answer no longer depends on the question: the walk
always runs over the window the temple is buying for, and `from`/`to` now only choose which of those
meals to report. `DayView` still asks about one day, deliberately — leaving it that way is what
demonstrates the fix is in the semantics rather than in a wider query.

## The comparison I used, and how a meal excludes its own claim

> **on hand, minus the claims of every other planned meal that reaches the pot before this one.**

Which is what the running walk already computed — a meal is never ahead of itself, so **its own claim
is never subtracted from the figure it is judged against.** That is the trap the brief names, and the
answer to it is structural rather than a special case: nothing needs to detect "this meal" and
subtract it back out.

**I did not use the symmetric shape the brief guessed at** (*on hand minus every other planned meal's
claim, ahead or behind*), and this is the one place I want a reader to stop, because it is a
deliberate departure from the brief's own sketch:

- Six days needing 10 kg each against 50 kg: every day is individually unaffordable once the other
  five are paid for, so **all six turn amber**. The brief's required test says the sixth is short and
  the first five are not — and it is right to. Six warnings where the temple has one problem is
  noise, and it cannot say which day the sack actually runs out on.
- It would also have failed the **existing** double-booking test (7 kg, two 5 kg meals): symmetric
  exclusion reads both as short, where the test — and the temple — expects the first covered and the
  second short by 3.

The queue is the cooking order (date, then ready-by, then created-at), which is the order the store
is genuinely drawn down in and the order the planner already shows. Rajeev's objection to widening
the range was that the answer depended on *where the day sat in an ordering nobody can see*; what
made that ordering invisible was that its **extent** was the accident — the range the screen happened
to ask for. The extent is now fixed and shared with the inventory screen, and the ordering that
remains is the cooking order on the page.

**Same recipe on two days:** each claim is a row keyed by meal-plan id, so the later one is charged
for the earlier and the earlier for neither. Tested at 15 kg with two 10 kg Khichdis — the first
reads ready, the second short by exactly 5, and each gives that answer when opened on its own.

## What I decided for the two edge cases

**A meal beyond the horizon: `PLANNING` — the badge makes no claim about stock.** It is claimed by
nobody, including itself, and the rice it will be cooked from has not been bought yet. Measuring it
against today's shelf would leave a Janmashtami plan red for three months for a reason nobody can
act on — the same argument T-086 used for bounding *committed* at all. As the festival comes inside
the window its claim appears, which is the same moment the shopping list starts buying for it.
**A past-dated meal still `PLANNED` gets the same answer for the same reason** (it is T-087's
recording gap, not a claim on the shelf), so there is one rule and not two: *if the plan is not one
of the claims that make up committed, the badge does not speak about stock for it.*

**A recorded meal: the badge does not speak for it, and never did.** `status = 'PLANNED'` bounds both
the walk and the report, so a cooked dish appears in neither — `MealServices` badges it "Cooked" from
the dish's own status before it ever consults sufficiency. Its stock has already moved through the
ledger; a second opinion here would subtract the same rice twice. The new test also proves the other
half: recording a meal **releases** its claim to the meals behind it.

**What the badge says now**, and why no wording changed: `SHORT` → "Short of ingredients" and
`SUFFICIENT` → "Ingredients ready" are both still true sentences under the new comparison (they were
already order-dependent — the walk is not new). `PLANNING` renders as the neutral "Planned", which is
the honest thing for a day outside the buying window. **One visible consequence for Rajeev to look
at:** a dish planned three months out used to show a green or red ingredient badge and now shows a
plain "Planned". That is intended, but it is a change he will see, and if he wants that day to say
something more explicit ("beyond the buying window") it is a wording decision and his.

## The duplicated horizon: collapsed, and it now lives in `CommittedStockService`

`SufficiencyService` no longer computes a window at all — not the constants, not the festival
extension, not even a call to a shared helper. It receives the window **as the set of claims**, via
a new `CommittedStockService.claimsInHorizon()`, which hands out the same claims
`committedBaseByIngredient()` sums, un-summed and in planning order. So `BASE_HORIZON_DAYS = 14` and
`FESTIVAL_LOOKAHEAD_DAYS = 30` exist once, in `CommittedStockService`, and the planner badge and the
inventory *committed* column cannot disagree about which meals count — not by a day of festival
lookahead, and not by an exclusion added to one and forgotten in the other. `SufficiencyService`
also loses its `RecipeService`, `OccasionService` and `TempleClock` dependencies, because the claims
arrive already scaled.

One new public type, `CommittedStockService.MealClaim` — **nested rather than a new file**, both
because a new file would widen the contract and because it means nothing outside the list it is
returned in: its position (the order dishes reach the pot) is half of what it says.

Cost: a single-day question now walks the fortnight rather than the day. That is one indexed query
over `meal_plans` plus recipe scaling memoised on `(recipeId, targetYield)` — a temple cooking the
same khichadi all week scales it once — against the previous three-dish walk. Measured indirectly:
`SufficiencyIT`'s eight tests run in 1.198s total.

## The shopping-list seam: still merely inelegant

`shortfallFeed()` is byte-for-byte the same contract it was. It reads the same meals (`PLANNED`,
today → horizon), scaled the same way by the same `RecipeService`, merged per dish the same way, in
the same order — the only change is that the walk is now shared with the badge instead of duplicated
beside it. So stream 1 of `ShoppingListService` is unchanged, and stream 2's `onHand` top-up behind
an `available` gate is exactly as inelegant as T-086 left it and **no more wrong than it was**. I did
not touch it; it is still T-090's seam. `ShoppingListIT` (5) and `HandAddedLineIT` (8) pass unchanged,
which is the evidence for that claim rather than my reading of it.
