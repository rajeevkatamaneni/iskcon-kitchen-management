# T-095 — a source-derived check that an error's next step names a door its own reader is permitted to open, plus the hand audit that check cannot replace

**Headline, before anything else:** the audit found **six more next steps of the same class**, none
of them caught by the check I built. Five are permission mismatches (`KMS-400043`, `KMS-400058`,
`KMS-400089`, `KMS-400109`, `KMS-400110`) and one is a state/ownership mismatch (`KMS-400119`).
Proposed wording for each is in **Findings** below. **I did not edit `ErrorCode.java` — it is
reserved.** The check ships green because it cannot see any of them, which is the honest measure of
it and is stated in the test's own javadoc, in the report it writes, and here.

## 1. The audit, as a list

I walked all **148** next steps in `ErrorCode.java` and sorted each into one of four shapes. The
mechanised half of this is reproducible: the test writes a per-code verdict to
`backend/build/reports/next-step-permission-audit.txt`.

### 1a. Names no door — 106 codes

"Check the highlighted fields and try again." · "Enter what was actually paid." · "There is nothing
to reinstate." A next step that describes a field to correct, a value to re-enter, or the fact that
there is nothing to do **cannot be a lie about a door**, because it names none. This is the large
majority and it is uninteresting, which is worth saying: the defect class is rare because most next
steps do not instruct at all.

### 1b. Delegates rather than instructs — 13 codes

`KMS-400019`, `400021`, `400022`, `400024`, `400025`, `400027`, `400028`, `400031`, `400033`,
`400065`, `400071`, `400083`, `400098`.

The reviewing session's observation holds and generalises: **a next step that names a person is
unfalsifiable in a way one that names an action is not.** "Ask your temple administrator to restore
access" cannot point at a screen that does not exist. But it *can* point at a person who cannot
help, and **that half is decidable** — so I built it as the second assertion. `KMS-400098`'s current
wording ("Ask a Temple Admin to record a correction…") and `KMS-400071`'s ("…ask a Temple Admin to
add it in temple settings") are both machine-checked green: the Temple Admin does hold
`CORRECT_RECORDED_MEAL` and `MANAGE_TEMPLE_SETTINGS`. The other eleven delegate in words the
matcher cannot tie to a permission, so they are hand-cleared only.

The one shape delegation does *not* protect against is the `KMS-400020` failure: naming a person
when **no person is needed** — that reader could join a temple themselves. No check proposed here
would have caught that, and I do not think one can.

### 1c. Instructs a door, and the door is open — 33 codes, 4 of them by machine

Machine-checked: `KMS-400052`, `400054`, `400137`, `400139`†, plus the two delegations above.

Hand-cleared, the interesting ones: `400015` (open the existing machine — `MANAGE_INVENTORY`, same
as the error), `400034` (add an alias — the field exists, `CreateIngredientRequest.aliases`),
`400042` (cook less / receive / adjust — all held by everyone who can hit it), `400081` (download
the export then delete — both `DELETE_TENANT`), `400084` (add the provider key under Settings —
raised only from `TenantPaymentSettingsService.test()` and `revealWebhookSecret`, both
`MANAGE_TEMPLE_SETTINGS`, so the reader is the right one), `400118`, `400129` ("Use *Did these
arrive?* on the order" — the control now exists, `frontend/app/orders/[id]/page.tsx:691`),
`400132` (credit note — `invoice/CreditInvoiceRequest.java` exists), `400133`/`400134` (already
cleared in the dispatch row; I re-derived both and agree).

† `KMS-400139` is checked green **and is wrong** — see finding 3. The matcher identified the wrong
door. It is the clearest single illustration of this check's limit and I would rather it stood in
the record than be quietly excluded.

### 1d. Instructs a door the reader cannot open — 6 codes

All six below. **Three failure modes were proposed in the dispatch row; the audit found instances of
two of them** (permission ×5, state ×1) and no new instance of "never built" — `KMS-400129`, the one
that had it, has since been given a real control.

## 2. Findings, with the sentence I would write

I cannot edit `ErrorCode.java`. Each of these is a one-line change to the fourth constructor
argument.

### Finding 1 — `KMS-400043` `EQUIPMENT_SCRAPPED` · permission

- **Today:** "Register a replacement, or reinstate this item if it's back in use."
- **Who meets it:** `EquipmentService.changeCondition` (`EquipmentService.java:177`), reached from
  `POST /api/v1/equipment/{id}/condition`, `@PreAuthorize("hasAuthority('MANAGE_INVENTORY')")` —
  Temple Admin, Kitchen Manager **and Kitchen Staff**.
- **The door:** reinstating is `POST /{id}/reinstate`,
  `@PreAuthorize("hasAuthority('REINSTATE_SCRAPPED_EQUIPMENT')")` — **Temple Admin alone**, and
  `Permission.java` says the narrowing is deliberate (D-15).
- **So:** two of the three roles who can meet this error are told to do something they cannot. Half
  the sentence ("register a replacement") is fine, which is why it reads well.
- **Proposed:** *"Register a replacement, or ask a Temple Admin to reinstate it if it's back in
  use."*

### Finding 2 — `KMS-400058` `SHIFT_NOT_OPEN` · permission

- **Today:** "Cancelled shifts can't be changed or signed up for. Post a new shift instead."
- **Who meets it:** `SignupService.guardOpenAndFuture` (`SignupService.java:528`), called from
  `signUp` (:64) and `joinWaitlist` (:342), both reached from `VolunteerShiftController` behind
  `SIGN_UP_FOR_SHIFTS` — **which only a Volunteer holds**.
- **The door:** posting a shift is `POST /api/v1/shifts` behind `MANAGE_VOLUNTEER_SHIFTS`, which a
  Volunteer does not hold.
- **So:** a devotee who taps a shift that was cancelled while they were looking at it is told to
  post one themselves. This is the same defect as `KMS-400098`, in the epic furthest from it.
- **Proposed:** *"Look for another shift, or ask whoever runs the roster to post a new one."*

### Finding 3 — `KMS-400139` `ATTENDANCE_ALREADY_RECORDED` · permission

- **Today:** "Change the mark on the shift's roster, beside the volunteer's name."
- **Who meets it:** `SignupService.java:184`, reached from `POST /api/v1/shifts/{id}/attendance`
  behind `MANAGE_VOLUNTEER_SHIFTS` — Temple Admin, Kitchen Manager **and Kitchen Staff**.
- **The door:** `PUT /api/v1/shifts/{id}/attendance/{userId}` behind
  `CORRECT_RECORDED_ATTENDANCE` — Temple Admin and Kitchen Manager only.
  `RolePermissions.java` is explicit that excluding Kitchen Staff is the point of T-106: *"a cook
  standing beside the volunteers on that roster should not be able to change a record about one of
  them."*
- **So:** the cook who marked the shift is the single most likely reader of this error, and is the
  one role the next step is wrong for. **This is `KMS-400098` again — same product, same wave-worth
  of work, one epic across.**
- **Proposed:** *"A Kitchen Manager or Temple Admin can change the mark on the shift's roster,
  beside the volunteer's name."*

### Finding 4 — `KMS-400109` `KITCHEN_ARCHIVED` · permission (partial)

- **Today:** "Restore it from the kitchens list first, or pick a different one."
- **Who meets it:** `IngredientRequestService.java:407`, in `resolveKitchen`, reached from create,
  update and submit behind `REQUEST_INGREDIENTS` — Temple Admin, Kitchen Manager and Kitchen Staff.
  (It is also raised in `KitchenService.java:318` behind `MANAGE_KITCHENS`, where the wording is
  right.)
- **The door:** restoring a kitchen is `MANAGE_KITCHENS` — **Temple Admin alone**.
- **Proposed:** *"Pick a different kitchen, or ask a Temple Admin to restore this one."*

### Finding 5 — `KMS-400110` `KITCHEN_PLANS_ITS_OWN_MEALS` · permission (partial)

- **Today:** "Pick a kitchen that only asks for ingredients, or turn the meal planner off for this
  one."
- Same throw site (`IngredientRequestService.java:410`), same audience; turning the planner off is
  `MANAGE_KITCHENS`, Temple Admin alone.
- **Proposed:** *"Pick a kitchen that only asks for ingredients, or ask a Temple Admin to turn the
  meal planner off for this one."*

### Finding 6 — `KMS-400089` `LEAVE_OVERLAPS_EXISTING` · permission (partial), and the wrong voice

- **Today:** "Open their leave and change the existing record, or choose dates that don't overlap."
- **Who meets it:** `LeaveService.refuseIfOverlapping` (:376), called from `recordOnBehalf` (:191,
  `APPROVE_LEAVE`) **and from `request` (:139, `REQUEST_OWN_LEAVE`)** — which every temple-side role
  holds, Kitchen Staff included.
- **The door:** changing an existing leave record is `APPROVE_LEAVE`, which Kitchen Staff do not
  hold. The pronoun gives it away: a cook asking for their own leave is being addressed as
  *"their"*.
- **Proposed:** *"Choose dates that don't overlap, or ask whoever approves leave to change the
  existing record."*

### Finding 7 — `KMS-400119` `INGREDIENT_REQUEST_NOT_SUBMITTED` · **state / ownership**, not permission

- **Today:** "Open it and send it for review, then it can be approved or turned down."
- **Who meets it:** `wrongStatus` (`IngredientRequestService.java:704`), raised when somebody
  approves, denies or withdraws a request still in draft — so the reader is usually the **approver**
  (`APPROVE_INGREDIENT_REQUESTS`).
- **The door:** `submit` (:203) calls `requireDraft` and then `isAuthor`, and refuses a non-author
  with `KMS-400112` — *"only the person who wrote it can change it."*
- **So:** the approver holds no permission that is missing; the ownership guard refuses them
  anyway. **This is the `KMS-400143` shape** — the next step names a door that exists, that the
  reader may in principle open, and that this reader specifically cannot. It is the mode the
  dispatch row warned was not mechanisable, and I agree: nothing in the source says "submit is
  author-only" in a form a test could read without reading Java control flow.
- **Proposed:** *"Whoever raised it has to send it for review first."*

## 3. What the check actually covers

`backend/src/test/java/org/iskcon/kms/error/NextStepPermissionTest.java`. Six tests.

**It is named for what it does.** `NextStepPermissionTest`, and the assertion is *"an instructing
next step names no door its own reader lacks the permission for"* — not `everyNextStepIsReachable`.
The class javadoc opens by saying it covers one of three failure modes and names the other two as
uncovered, and names `KMS-400043`, `KMS-400058` and `KMS-400139` as live defects it does not catch.

**Nothing is hand-listed.** The model is read out of the source tree on every run:

1. **Who meets each error.** Every controller method carrying a `@PreAuthorize` is walked into the
   methods it calls, within its own package, to depth 4; every `ErrorCode.X` reachable is attributed
   to that method's permission. 122 of the 144 codes referenced in `src/main` get an audience this
   way. Method bodies are found by brace matching, and comments are stripped first — `RolePermissions`
   and `Permission` explain at length which permissions a role *does not* hold, and reading those
   sentences as code would invert the policy.
2. **Which doors are in the same feature area.** The permissions the package's controllers declare.
3. **Which door the sentence names.** The permission constant's own name is its vocabulary —
   `CORRECT_RECORDED_MEAL` is the words *correct*, *record*, *meal*. Two must appear in the sentence
   and at most one may be missing, the winner must be unique, and only **imperative clauses** are
   read (a clause carrying a modal or negation is stating a rule, not instructing).

Then: **every role that can meet the error holds the permission the next step names**, and its
mirror for delegation — "ask a Temple Admin to X" is checked against whether a Temple Admin holds X.

**Coverage, stated plainly: 6 of 148 codes.** The report the test writes gives the reason for each of
the other 142 — 106 "names no door of this area in the area's own words", 29 "no instruction in this
sentence", 7 "no guarded endpoint in this feature area". A reader who takes a green run for an
all-clear has ignored a file the test wrote for them.

**Why it is still worth having:** it is a guard on codes added *from here*. The two shapes it decides
are the two that recur — an instruction whose door is narrower than the error's own guard, and a
delegation to somebody who cannot help. Both are cheap, both are now permanent, and both were
invisible to `ErrorCodeTest`, which has 148 green parameterised assertions per property and could not
see any of the six defects that prompted this task.

**What it cannot do, and why not:**

- **State (mode 2).** Not attempted. `KMS-400119` above is a fresh instance and shows why: the
  refusal is an `isAuthor` check three statements into a service method. Deciding it needs the state
  machine, and the state machine is Java control flow.
- **Never built (mode 3).** Not attempted, and I do not think it is reachable from the backend alone
  — `KMS-400129`'s missing door was a missing *control*, which lives in `frontend/app`.
- **A door named in words the permission does not use.** Findings 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6 all fail here.
  "Post a new shift", "change the mark", "restore it from the kitchens list", "open their leave" —
  a person reads all four instantly and the matcher reads none of them, because `MANAGE_KITCHENS`
  does not contain the word *restore*. **Teaching it those synonyms is precisely the hand-kept list
  that was ruled out**, and I think that ruling is right: the synonym list would be correct on the
  day it was written and would rot exactly as `terraform.tfvars.example` did.
- **A door in another feature area.** Candidates come only from the packages the code is thrown in.
  Searching all 39 permissions made ties far more common than hits.
- **False negatives from clause filtering.** A clause misjudged as a statement is skipped silently.
  This is deliberate — the alternative produced two false alarms in prototyping (`KMS-400059` and
  `KMS-400099`, both restatements the matcher read as instructions) and a check that cries wolf is
  worse than a thin one.

**Vacuity guards, because every assertion here is "nothing was found wrong".** A parser that stops
matching after a refactor produces a green run with no work done — the failure shape this project
has a rule about. `theModelThisCheckRestsOnIsActuallyPopulated` asserts that more than 25 packages
yielded controller permissions, that more than 100 codes traced back to an endpoint, and that at
least 4 next steps were actually decided. If the scan goes dark, the suite goes red rather than
green.

## 5. The negative control

Full transcript in `docs/work/proof/control-T-095.log`.

A fixture — **not a change to `ErrorCode.java`** — was fed through the same loop the real enum goes
through:

```java
check(new Subject("Reinstate the scrapped equipment.", Set.of("equipment"),
        Set.of("MANAGE_INVENTORY")), model);
```

Everything but the sentence is real and read from the tree. The check caught it:

```
NextStepPermissionTest > an instructing next step names no door its own reader lacks the permission for FAILED
    java.lang.AssertionError: [each of these tells its reader to open a door their own permissions do not open — the shape of KMS-400098. Reword the next step, or widen the permission; do not widen this test]
    Expecting empty but was: ["KMS-999001 SHUT           REINSTATE_SCRAPPED_EQUIPMENT     reachable by [KITCHEN_MANAGER, KITCHEN_STAFF] who do NOT hold REINSTATE_SCRAPPED_EQUIPMENT"]

  Total:    6
  Passed:   5
  Failed:   1
  Result:   FAILURE
```

The injection was reverted and the suite returned to 6/6. The fixture survives as the permanent test
*"the check catches a next step whose door the reader cannot open"*, with a **positive half** beside
it — *"the same sentence behind the right permission is left alone"* — because without it a check
that flagged the sentence unconditionally would have looked equally caught. A third control covers
the delegation assertion ("Ask a Temple Admin to sign up for the shift" — a Temple Admin
deliberately does not hold `SIGN_UP_FOR_SHIFTS`).

**The control is honest about being sharpened.** It is `KMS-400043`'s real defect restated in the
permission's own vocabulary. The real sentence — "Register a replacement, or reinstate this item if
it's back in use." — scores one matching word where two are required, so **the check does not catch
the defect the control is modelled on**. That gap is the finding, not a flaw in the control.

## 6. Not done / needs Rajeev

1. **Seven wording changes to `ErrorCode.java`**, sections 2 above. I did not make them; the file is
   reserved. Five are permission mismatches of the `KMS-400098` shape, one is a state/ownership
   mismatch of the `KMS-400143` shape, and one (`KMS-400139`) is arguably the sharpest instance yet
   found, because the role it is wrong for is the role most likely to be reading it.
2. **The check will not go green→red when those are fixed, and will not go red now.** It cannot see
   any of the seven. If you want it to, the only mechanism I can see is a synonym table from English
   verbs to permissions, which is the hand-kept list T-095 ruled out. **I recommend against it** and
   would rather the class stayed small and honest.
3. **No hand smoke-test.** This task adds no user-facing surface — it is a test class and two
   documents — so Commandment 5's manual pass does not apply. Nothing was deployed and nothing on
   staging changed.
4. **Two prototyping false alarms worth knowing about**, in case the clause filter is ever loosened:
   `KMS-400059` ("You can only sign up for or release a shift before it begins") and `KMS-400099`
   ("A cancelled meal never went to the kitchen…") both matched a permission before imperative
   filtering was added. Neither is a defect; both restate a rule rather than instruct.
5. **The audience walk is approximate and says so in its javadoc.** It follows calls by name inside
   one package. It over-reaches where two classes in a package share a method name (widening an
   audience, which can only produce a finding a human then reads) and under-reaches where a package
   calls across to another (leaving a code unchecked and named in the report). 122 of 144 codes
   resolved; the 22 that did not are listed as "no guarded endpoint in this feature area" or fall out
   of the audience map. A real call graph would need a Java parser as a test dependency, which I did
   not think this earned.
6. **`git status` at hand-off** is dirty with three builders' work, as expected in a shared
   checkout. **Mine are exactly three paths** — `backend/src/test/java/org/iskcon/kms/error/NextStepPermissionTest.java`,
   `docs/work/proof/T-095.md`, `docs/work/proof/control-T-095.log`. The rest (T-104's
   `CommunicationSendGuardSourceTest`, T-110's `V117` and donation receipt, T-122's `V116` and
   inventory changes) are not mine and I did not open them. No Flyway migration ran during my
   verification: this test starts no Spring context and needs no Docker, which is why it finishes in
   three seconds and cannot be broken by a neighbour's migration.
