## What was built

`BuyingAmount.of(needed, unit, vendorPack, ingredientPacks)` decides the computed quantity. First match wins:

1. **Preferred vendor's "Sells it as" pack** (`vendor_supplies.pack_size_id`): that pack only, `ceil(need / pack)`. 100 Kg with Bag = 25 Kg → 4 × Bag (25 Kg); 101 Kg → 5 bags (never 4 bags and a 5 Kg). `packFromVendor = true`.
2. **Ingredient's pack sizes** (R-ING-1), when there is no vendor pack. Rule below.
3. **Step table** otherwise (one `STEPS` list, commented as Rajeev's rule of 2026-09-19): under 1 kg/L → next 50 g/ml; 1–10 → next ½; 10–100 → next 1 kg; over 100 → next 5 kg; pieces → next whole number. The boundaries belong to the band below (1 Kg, 10 Kg and 100 Kg stay as they are).

A typed quantity never goes through `of`. `describeTyped` returns the typed figure unchanged. It fills `buyPacks` only when the figure is an exact whole number of the vendor's pack (75 Kg → 3 × Bag (25 Kg); 90 Kg → empty).

`ShoppingListLineView` gained `List<BuyPackView> buyPacks, boolean packFromVendor` at the end. `BuyPackView(UUID packSizeId, String label, BigDecimal perPackQty, int count)` is exactly the given shape. `perPackQty` is in the line's unit, and `suggestedQty` equals the sum of count × perPackQty (tested by a sweep).

### The exact pack rule (clarifier + conductor ruling)

- The clarifier said ordering and mixing were **NOT SETTLED**. It said nothing caps the leftover, and the step table is only for ingredients with no packs.
- **The conductor ruled (2026-09-19, binding for this build):** least left over first; on a tie, fewer packs. **Mixing sizes is allowed as a provisional default** pending Rajeev's Q-16. It is a switch, `BuyingAmount.MIX_PACK_SIZES`, which sits beside `STEPS` and is commented as provisional.
- The rule: pick the combination whose total ≥ need with the least left over. Of those, pick the fewest packs. On a true tie, keep the larger packs. It is implemented as an exact coin search over multiples of the packs' common divisor. The search only looks at totals below need + the smallest pack.
- Worked examples (all in `BuyingAmountTest`):
  - Tea 416 g, {250 g, 500 g, 1 Kg}: 2×250 and 1×500 both leave 84 g, so fewer packs wins → **1 × 500 g** (either setting).
  - 1,200 g, same packs, **mixed**: 1 Kg + 250 g (50 left, 2 packs) beats 5 × 250 g (50 left, 5 packs) → **1 × 1 Kg + 1 × 250 gm**.
  - 1,200 g, **single size**: 5 × 250 (50 left) beats 3 × 500 (300) and 2 × 1 Kg (800) → **5 × 250 gm**.
  - 1,000 g: **1 × 1 Kg**, not 2 × 500 g.
- Safety valve: past 2,000,000 search slots (only a tiny pack against a huge need, e.g. a 1 mg pack against a tonne), the single-size answer is used instead. That answer is still never short.

### Judgement calls

- **Label.** No existing helper writes "Bag (25 Kg)". `PackSizeService.label` is package-private and writes the chip form, "Bag = 25 Kg". I built the label from `Quantities.exact` (the same call that helper uses) as `name + " (" + size + ")"`, or the size alone for an unnamed pack. The method is called `asWritten()`, not `label()`, because `UnitLabelAgreementTest` flags any `.label()` call. It went red on my first draft for exactly that, and again on a comment that quoted the call.
- **Pack sizes are read with the existing public `PackSizeService.allByIngredient()`.** That is one query for the whole list. The vendor pack id is added to the existing preferred-vendor query, so it costs no extra statement. Net: +1 statement per page load. `ShoppingListStatementCountIT` counts per caller, not in total, so nothing broke. I added a soft assertion that `PackSizeService` sends exactly 1 statement per list read, with the reason in a comment.
- The `perPackQty` and quantity conversion is exact (no 3-decimal rounding), so the sum always matches.
