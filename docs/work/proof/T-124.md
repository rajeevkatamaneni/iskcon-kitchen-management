# T-124 — on-time delivery is scored per item, and a vendor who never turned up is named

Rajeev's five delivery scenarios of 2026-09-09 now score the figures he gave them. On-time is the
mean of per-item fractions instead of a binary read of the first arrival, a cancellation can say
"Vendor Never Delivered this Order" and be scored zero for it, and a `NOT_DELIVERED` return takes
its quantity back out of the on-time count — which is how **T-109 dissolves**, with no order-grain
rule for the partial case.

**One thing in the brief could not be built as written, and it is not the checkbox — see
"Not done / needs Rajeev".**

## Acceptance criteria

**Rajeev's five cases**, each a named test in `VendorPerformanceIT`, each asserting his figure:

| | test | asserts |
|---|---|---|
| A | `rajeevsCaseA_everythingInsideTheWindow` | `onTimePercent` 100, 10 of 10 items |
| B | `rajeevsCaseB_splitAcrossDaysInsideTheWindow` | `onTimePercent` 100 across two receipts |
| C | `rajeevsCaseC_eightOfTenItemsInTheWindow` | `onTimePercent` **80**, `itemsOnTime` 8 of 10, and `fillRatePercent` still 100 — which is why fill could never have caught this |
| D | `rajeevsCaseD_nothingInsideTheWindow` | `onTimePercent` 0, fill 100 |
| E | `rajeevsCaseE_neverDeliveredIsNamedAsANoShow` | `onTimePercent` 0, `abandonedOrders` 1, and the order judged with `neededBy` still **in the future** |

**The arithmetic**, all six points of the ledger row:

1. Per line, on-time quantity ÷ ordered, clamped to 1 — `itemFraction`, and
   `overDeliveryIsClampedAtAHundred` proves 48-of-40 does not pay for a line that never came.
2. Per order, the mean of its line fractions — `OrderScore.mean()`.
3. Per vendor, the mean of its order scores — `Totals.onTimePercent()`, a running sum divided at the
   end so the totals row never averages averages.
4. Described lines binary off `arrived_on` — `aDescribedLineIsScoredYesOrNo`, and
   `aDescribedLineCountsAsOneItemBesideTheRice` shows one counting once beside a catalogue line.
5. `NOT_DELIVERED` removes that quantity — `aNeverDeliveredReturnComesOffTheOnTimeCount`, with
   `aVendorFaultReturnThatIsNotAKeyingErrorLeavesOnTimeAlone` as its control.
6. Cancelled-and-abandoned scores 0 and enters the count; cancelled without the tick scores nothing
   at all — `rajeevsCaseE_…` and `anUntickedCancellationScoresNothingEitherWay`, which asserts the
   unticked vendor is **absent from the report entirely** rather than scored either way.

**The checkbox.** `"Vendor Never Delivered this Order"`, Rajeev's wording, unchanged. Unticked by
default (`useState(false)`), reset every time the panel is toggled, with one plain line under it:
*"This counts against the vendor's delivery record. Leave it alone if we are cancelling for our own
reasons."* The reason field is still `required`. Three frontend tests cover the default, the
false/true payload, and that a tick cannot survive from one cancellation into the next.

**The trail.** `purchase_orders.vendor_abandoned` (V118), the flag in the `PO_CANCELLED` audit
after-state, and a sentence appended to the CANCELLED event on the order's own activity trail —
`tickingTheBoxNamesTheVendor` asserts all three, and that `cancel_reason` survives verbatim.

**The two decisions the ledger row asked to be recorded** are written into the service's class
comment, in the paragraphs that used to argue for the binary rule: *"The fill rate does not move"*
and *"An abandoned cancellation … is judged the moment it is cancelled"*. The fill-rate and
return-reason reasoning from T-103 and T-024 is left intact and untouched.

**Migration.** V118 only. `ls` confirmed the tree's highest was V117 before I started.

## Not done / needs Rajeev

### 1. "The receipt number the on-time quantity came from" cannot be built — there is no receipt number

The ledger row asks for three things beside the percentage. **Two are built** — quantity-within-line
as *"8 of 10 items across 3 orders"*, and abandoned as its own count, *"2 orders never delivered"*.
The third has no referent in this product:

- **Goods receipts have no number.** `goods_receipts` (V27) carries `id`, `po_id`,
  `idempotency_key`, `delivery_note_ref` (a GCS object name for a scanned delivery note), `note`,
  `received_by`, `received_at`. `GoodsReceiptView` exposes no number either. The only
  `receipt_number` anywhere in the tree is `donations.receipt_number`, added by V117 for 80G
  receipts — a different thing entirely.
- **And the screen has no grain to put one at.** The vendor performance table is one row per
  supplier over a period. A supplier's on-time quantity comes from every receipt against every
  order they had that month; there is no single receipt to name.

I did not invent a number and I did not invent a place to put one. **What I think he actually wants,
and the cheapest way to give it to him:** the purchase-order detail screen already lists an order's
lines and its receipts, and that *is* the grain where "which delivery did this quantity come from"
is a real question — a per-line "8 of 10 kg in time, received 12 Sep" on the order screen. That is a
new surface rather than a line of text, so it wants his word before it is built. **Please put it to
him as: do you want the delivery date beside each line on the order screen, or did you mean
something else by "receipt number"?**

### 2. The tick is not read back on the order screen

A cancelled order's screen shows `Cancelled: {reason}` and the activity trail, which now carries
*"… — recorded as never delivered by the vendor."* — so the fact **is** visible. What it does not
have is a badge on the header.

That was a deliberate stop at the contract line. Showing it as its own field means adding
`vendorAbandoned` to `PurchaseOrderView` in `api.ts`, and that interface is constructed by
`frontend/__tests__/invoice-order-picker.test.tsx:90`, which is **outside my contract** — a
required-and-nullable field (this repo's convention, and the right one) would fail `tsc` in a file
another builder may be in. The trail line was the honest way to make the fact readable without
reaching into somebody else's file. If a badge is wanted, it is a one-line follow-up plus that test
file granted to whoever takes it.

### 3. One judgement call in the arithmetic that Rajeev's five cases do not settle

**Rejected quantity counts as having arrived.** A sack refused at the gate was at the gate on the
day, so it scores on-time and does not score fill. This is the half of the old rule I kept, and it
keeps the two figures answering different questions — but none of his five scenarios involves a
rejection, so it is my reading of his intent rather than his instruction. It is pinned by
`rejectedQuantityCountsAsHavingArrived` and argued in the class comment, so reversing it is one test
and one SQL term. Worth one sentence to him.

### 4. Not smoke-tested by hand

Commandment 5 wants a new user-facing surface driven by hand. **I did not do that**, and cannot
honestly claim it: the change is not deployed, and a builder does not deploy. The checkbox and both
screens are covered by rendering tests that press the real controls (open the cancel panel, tick the
box, submit the form, read the payload), but that is not the same as somebody cancelling a real
order on staging and then opening the vendor performance report to see the vendor named. **That
needs to happen on the next deploy before this is called done.**

### 5. Two existing tests changed meaning, deliberately, and say so in their own comments

`aPartDeliveryScoresThePartThatCame` (was `punctualButShortIsVisibleAsBoth`, 100% → 25%) and
`aDescribedLineCountsAsOneItemBesideTheRice` (was `theEarlierOfTheTwoArrivalsIsWhatCounts`, 100% →
50%). Both carry a CHANGED ON PURPOSE block explaining what the old assertion claimed and why it is
now the wrong answer, in the style T-066 used on the same file. A third —
`aDescribedLineDoesNotDragTheFillRateDown` — kept every fill assertion it had and only changed **row
order**, because the report sorts worst-on-time first and the stools now move that column; its
comment says so, because a reordered test is exactly the kind of edit that looks like a weakened one.
