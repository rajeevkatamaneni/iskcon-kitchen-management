# T-037 — Registration remembers the credential it made and resumes at the join

A refused join no longer strands anybody. `createAccount()` keeps the credential the last press
made and the next press picks up at `api.joinTemple` instead of asking Firebase for a second
account it will refuse to give — so the second press no longer produces `auth/email-already-in-use`
and no longer answers with "Sign in instead.", which was advice that was wrong for exactly this
person.

## What was built

One guard in front of the credential step, not three fixes, because Google, password and phone are
inline branches of the one `createAccount()`:

- `madeCredential` — a `useRef<User | null>` holding the credential an earlier press made. It is
  set **before** `api.joinTemple` is called, so the identity outlives the refusal.
- `credentialToJoinWith(auth)` — returns the remembered credential if there is one, and otherwise
  runs the branch the chosen method selects. The three method branches moved into it unchanged,
  comments and all.
- The remembered credential is reused only while the email still matches it. A phone credential
  carries no email, so "no email" counts as still the same person; a mismatch means they edited the
  address after the failure, which is a different identity and gets made rather than reused.
- `firebaseCode()` extracted out of `readableFirebaseError()` so `isEmailAlreadyInUse()` can share
  it. `readableFirebaseError()` itself is unchanged in behaviour, including the string at what was
  line 348.

Nothing was deleted, no error code was added, `lib/api.ts`, `lib/auth-context.tsx` and
`lib/firebase.ts` were read but not touched, and the fix never reaches into the auth context.

## Why the other two shapes were not built

**Compensating-delete the Firebase user.** The most destructive of the three and the hardest to get
right: the delete can itself fail, and then somebody is worse off than the bug leaves them —
account gone, or half gone, mid-registration. It also assumes the account is not legitimately
theirs, which is exactly the assumption that produced the wrong "Sign in instead." in the first
place. Rejected on Rajeev's own reasoning; nothing in the built fix deletes anything.

**Reserve the temple first, create the credential last.** The largest change and probably not
buildable at all. `POST /api/v1/temples/{id}/join` needs an authenticated uid, and
`AuthenticationFilter` (`backend/.../AuthenticationFilter.java:123,129-134`) admits a verified uid
with *no membership* precisely so this flow works — so the identity genuinely has to exist before
the join can be attempted. Inverting the order would mean a new unauthenticated reservation
endpoint, i.e. backend work, which this task's contract explicitly excludes and which the server is
right not to need.

**Why the chosen shape is the right one.** It matches what actually happened: their identity is
fine and only the membership is missing. It deletes nothing, adds no endpoint, and is the same
shape as the claim-on-match design already in the product.

## The reload case — what I chose, and why

A reload loses `madeCredential`, so the in-session memory alone would leave a reloaded person back
at the wrong copy. **What I built: on `auth/email-already-in-use` with nothing remembered, look at
`auth.currentUser`; if it is signed in with the same email that was typed, that is the stranded
credential and we resume at the join with it. If it is nobody, or somebody else, the original error
is re-thrown and "There is already an account with that email. Sign in instead." stands.**

This works because the page deliberately does **not** sign out after a failed join (the existing
test asserted `firebaseSignOut` was not called, and it still does), and Firebase's session survives
a reload and a browser restart — so what was made is still signed in and findable.

**I did not build the recommended variant** — re-signing in with the same email and password on
`auth/email-already-in-use` — and the reasons are worth having on the record:

1. **It takes the right message away from the person it is right for.** Somebody who genuinely
   already has an account, is not mid-registration, and types their real password would be silently
   joined to a temple by the registration form instead of being told to sign in. The acceptance
   criterion says that person must still get "Sign in instead.", and with `currentUser` they always
   do; with the password retry they only do when they mistype.
2. **It makes the register screen a password-checking surface.** Being joined-or-not is an
   observable answer to "is this the password for this email". Sign-in is where that question
   belongs, and the register form gets no such answer under the built fix.
3. **It buys almost nothing extra.** The case it uniquely covers is somebody stranded who *also*
   lost their Firebase session — cleared site data, a closed private window, a different device.
   Everything short of that, tab close and browser restart included, `currentUser` already
   recovers.

**The sliver it leaves:** a stranded person who lost their Firebase session entirely still sees
"Sign in instead.", signs in, and is bounced by `RequireRole` to `/choose-temple` — recoverable,
but only by accident, exactly as T-022 described. Naming it rather than hiding it: closing it
properly is a "signed in, no membership, here is the temple you were joining" screen, which is a
different task and not in this contract.

The reload story is coherent for all three methods, which is why it lives only in the password
branch: after a reload the form is empty and the tab is back on Password, so a stranded Google or
phone person who fills the form in again lands on `createUserWithEmailAndPassword`, gets
`email-already-in-use` (one account per email), and is recovered by the same `currentUser` check.
If instead they pick their original tab, the Google popup or a fresh OTP re-authenticates them and
the join simply runs.
