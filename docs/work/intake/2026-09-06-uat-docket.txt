What I would do with this
The build order in the first section is the one being worked, smallest real thing first. Around it, four things matter more than the rest:
Wire up the correction endpoint. It closes the recorded-meal item and, with it, most of the nine “a mistake is permanent” findings, which all bottom out in the same missing screen. The hard part is written and tested.
Give the temple record an edit screen. A temple provisioned in the wrong timezone, or one that gains 80G approval, currently has to be deleted. Testers provision temples all day.
Fix the five controls that refuse the person looking at them. An hour's work, and each otherwise arrives as a bug report from every tester holding that role.
Decide, don't discover, on procurement. No manual PO, nothing non-food purchasable, nothing addable to the shopping list by hand. Whether that is release-one scope is a product call — write it down before a tester finds it as a bug.
 And one lesson worth carrying rather than filing: a stale document is worse than a
 missing one. Five of the twelve were wrong, and each cost more than an unrecorded gap
 would have — a tester told to work around a fixed defect, a decision re-made from a stale
 bullet, a redesign reported as unstarted while it sat finished on staging. Before anything on
 this page is scheduled, it is worth ten minutes against the code.
