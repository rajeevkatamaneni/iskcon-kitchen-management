Two things the review found that it wasn't looking for

Neither of these is anybody's comment. Both fell out of checking the comments against the code, and both matter more than most of what follows.

The documents promise a setting that does not exist

REQUIREMENTS.md:146 says the order list is computed "over a configurable horizon". EPIC-5 promises a safety factor and a lead buffer as "tenant config, default 1.2×" and "default 2 days". EPIC-4 promises "a configurable horizon (default: through 14 days + any festival within 30)".

None of it is configurable. All four numbers are private static final in Java — SufficiencyService.java:35-36 and OrderListService.java:44-45. A temple that buys weekly and one that buys monthly get fourteen days each. This is exactly the divergence our document discipline is supposed to prevent, and it got through.

There is no file import anywhere in the application

Not "no importer for invoices" — no upload of any kind. Zero occurrences of MultipartFile, of multipart/form-data, of <input type="file">; no multipart configuration at all. Data leaves — the donations CSV — and never arrives. Even the delivery-note scan on a goods receipt is a text box where somebody types the name of a file that nothing uploads.

Two comments walked into this independently, from two different departments. That is usually the sign of a real hole rather than a wish.

MP2

Auto-filter to the recipes we have the stock for

"A check box to Auto filter for getting only those recipes for which the groceries stock is / going to be present."

My read

The need is real. The mechanism is wrong, and I'd argue against it fairly hard. Three reasons, and the third is a defect rather than a preference.

  • Sufficiency isn't a property of a recipe. A meal is sufficient only after earlier uncooked meals in the horizon have taken their share — that's the double-booking guard, and it's the whole reason two meals can never both claim one sack of rice. "Can I cook this?" has no answer until you say how many servings, on what date, behind what else. A row in a picker cannot honestly carry a green tick.
  • Filtering hides the food you most need to see. If rice is short, hiding every rice dish means the planner never learns rice is short at the one moment they could act. They just find a shorter menu and no explanation.
  • It would starve the purchase plan. Planned-meal shortfall is stream one of three in OrderListService.java:102-105 — the thing that buys the rice. If you can't plan a meal you're short for, you never generate the demand that fixes the shortage.
The filter would quietly starve the ordering system that fixes the very shortage it's hiding. That's circular, and it would be very hard to spot from inside.

The fix

Reject the filter, keep the need, fold it into MP3: flag on the row, never hide the row. A coarse, clearly-indicative flag against current stock at the recipe's default yield. It needs the shared "scaled ingredient basket" that our own E3-S8 D5 already admits is duplicated between two services and ought to be extracted.

MP4

Price per meal, by category of preparation

"Need the ability to get the price-per-meal for various categories of preparation viz., public prasadam, inmate, festival, sunday-feast, etc."

My read

They're right about the granularity, and this one asks us to revisit a decision we locked. E3-S8 D3 says in as many words: "For the day, not per meal. A per-meal figure would need the same estimate cut three ways with nothing gained."

I think D3 is now wrong, for a specific reason: it was written to answer a different question. D3's question was "what is today's food costing us" — a number for the Today screen, where a daily total is exactly right. Their question is "what does a public-prasadam plate cost against a Sunday feast plate", which is a comparison. And a comparison between categories is precisely the thing one daily total can never give you.

Same data, a different question — and the answer to the second one is not a worse version of the first.

Note what does not need revisiting. D1 — an estimate, honestly labelled, because much of the store is donated and a gift in kind has a value and no price — stays right. D4 — labour deliberately absent, because a cook on a 6am–2pm shift is making breakfast and lunch and their pay can only be allocated, never measured — stays right, and it also answers INV2.

The granularity isn't blocked by any of it. MaterialsCostService.mealsOn(date) already builds the per-meal baskets and throws the split away before returning one total. And the categories they named are the categories meal_kinds already holds: Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner, Deity offering, Catering order, Outside event, Festival feast.

The fix

Extract the shared scaled-basket code, key it by meal plan, cost each basket, aggregate by meal kind over a period, divide by servings. New screen. Every figure carries "estimated, materials only" and the count of unpriced ingredients, exactly as the daily one does. Changing D3 needs Rajeev's sign-off and a changelog entry.

INV1

Moving-average cost, from the incoming invoices

"It is not only for data analytics but is also important for budgeting as well as for the pricing of the external cooking orders… ERP has this feature out of the box."

My read

The strongest comment in the document, and the one I most want to agree with. There's one piece of sand in the framing, and I think the answer we should give is better than the one they asked for.

Where they're right — and I won't defend us

vendor_supplies.last_price is typed by hand and has exactly one writer in the entire codebase. Nothing in receiving or invoicing ever writes a price back. So the number ages the moment somebody stops maintaining it, and nothing notices or says so.

Checked
  • VendorService.java:131-145 — setSupply, the only writer
  • ReceivingService.java — grep price returns nothing
  • goods_receipt_lines — quantities, batch, expiry. No price column
  • VendorInvoiceService.java:55 — one write, and it's the insert

Their two use cases are the right ones, too. Budgeting, and pricing external catering orders. We take money for catering. Pricing that off a stale hand-typed figure is how a temple loses money quietly — a much better argument than the analytics one they led with.

Where the sand is

"The incoming invoices can be input and will be the basis" describes a switch we don't have. We hold the invoice as a document; we hold no price on any line of it. ERP has this out of the box because ERP makes the storekeeper type a rate on every goods receipt line. Somebody types it either way — the question is who and when, and we should say so rather than let "out of the box" imply it's free.

What I'd build instead — and this is a real disagreement worth putting to them

Step one: capture the price where the goods arrive. Add unit_price to goods_receipt_lines, prefilled from the purchase order's expected_price — which we already carry — and editable by whoever is receiving. Write it back. That alone kills the staleness, needs no valuation model at all, and is the prerequisite for everything else on this page.

Step two: cost at the batch, not at an average. We already track batches, because FEFO needs them — we know which batch every issue and every consumption came out of, because the allocator chose it.

Costing a draw at its own batch's price isn't an approximation of a moving average. It's more accurate than one, and it needs no averaging model at all. ERPs reach for a moving average because they've lost track of which lot the food came from. We haven't.

That also makes MP4 exact for bought food rather than estimated, which is a bigger prize than it looks. The one thing a batch price can't do is value a donation, and it shouldn't try — the report says how much of the basket that was, which is what we already do with unpriced ingredients today.

INV2

Utilities and maintenance in the price per meal

"Need to consider the utilities like Gas, Water, Electricity and Maintenance expenses like cleaning and operating supplies to calculate price per meal."

My read

True, and it's the same problem as labour, which E3-S8 D4 already refused with a reason I still think holds: a gas bill is allocated, never measured. Nobody can know how much gas the Sunday feast burned. You can only divide the month's bill by something — meals, servings, cooking hours — and every divisor is an assumption wearing the clothes of a measurement.

But I wouldn't stop where D4 stops, because there's a defensible middle it didn't consider: a fully-absorbed figure that shows its own working.

₹41 per serving — ₹28 materials, ₹9 utilities allocated by servings, ₹4 labour allocated by rostered hours.

That's honest in exactly the way D4 demands. It says which part is measured and which part is shared out, and it names the rule. It's also the number you actually need to price a catering order — the use case that makes this worth doing at all.

What has to be settled first

There is no expense ledger of any kind in the schema. No utilities, no maintenance, no operating supplies, nothing. So this is two pieces of work: build a small expense capture nobody has asked for yet, then build an allocation on top of it. And it must come after INV1 and MP4 — allocating overheads onto a materials figure that is itself a hand-typed estimate is precision laid over guesswork. It would make the number look more trustworthy while making it no more true.

INV3

Wholesale market prices, through agentic AI

"Current price from the wholesale market thru agentic AI."

My read

I'd decline this, and not on cost grounds. Ask what the number would be used for, because it has three possible jobs and it's wrong for two of them.

  • Valuing stock we already hold — wrong. Stock is worth what we paid. If market price fed the valuation, our food cost would move on a day when nobody bought anything and nobody cooked anything, and somebody would spend an afternoon working out why.
  • Pricing a catering order — a reasonable input, but a human sets the margin. It belongs beside the decision, not inside the ledger.
  • Checking whether our vendor is charging fairly — genuinely useful, and a completely different feature from the one described. An advisory, not a source.

There's also a sourcing problem the phrase "agentic AI" skates over. Indian wholesale prices are published per mandi, per grade, per day, and the grade is what sets the price. An agent that browses for "rice price" returns a number with an error bar wider than the thing we're trying to measure — and it would write that number into a financial record with no human in between.

That is where I'd expect this project's first serious data-quality incident.

What I'd offer instead

A price advisory on the ingredient screen: last paid, when, and a market reference with its source and date — read-only, never feeding the ledger. If a feed is wanted, take it from Agmarknet, which is government-published, per-mandi, per-grade and citable, rather than from an agent reading web pages.

I'd push backM · advisory only
INV4

Cost centres rather than kitchens

"Instead of calling the meal-category as kitchen, we can call it as a cost-center… Deity Kitchen may be a cost-center and whatever the main kitchen store issues will be against this cost-center."

My read

The premise is a misreading of the demo. The conclusion is half right, and the right half is worth building.

The misreading. We don't call the meal category a kitchen. They're two separate things and both already exist. A meal_kind is Breakfast, Lunch, Deity offering, Catering order, Outside event, Festival feast — the category of preparation. A kitchen is a physical place with a door, a person in charge, a phone number and a location, and its one behavioural flag is whether it plans its own meals. Our own design says it outright: is_main is a label; uses_meal_planner is the behaviour. There's nothing here to rename.

Where they're right, and we've missed it. An issue to a kitchen is already a cost attribution and we aren't using it as one. Every ISSUE movement records which kitchen it went to. "Whatever the store issues will be against this cost centre" is not a design change — it's a report nobody has written.

And notice what it does that per-meal costing can't. The Deity kitchen doesn't use the meal planner — that's the whole point of one kitchen, one door. So its cost can only be measured through issues. Two doors, two costing paths, one report.

What I'd resist is a third noun. A "cost centre" entity alongside kitchen and meal kind would have to be created, maintained and mapped, and on day one it would map one-to-one onto kitchens. Let the kitchen be the cost centre until somebody can name a case where the two differ.

The fix

A cost-by-kitchen report over ISSUE movements, valued however everything else is valued — and it becomes accurate for nothing extra once INV1's first step lands.

INV5

The Deity Kitchen buys some of its own things

"Deity Kitchen menu is dynamic and sometimes mathajis procure and get the items themselves apart from the main store supply."

My read

Correct, and our design already accepts it on purpose. Issuing takes food off the temple's books, and what the kitchen does afterwards is its own business — a kitchen that doesn't run this application won't record its own purchases in it, and giving it a second stock balance would produce a fiction within a month.

INVOICE1

Import payments from a file Accounts sends

"Payments are handled by the Accounts department. It would be helpful if we have a data import utility from a data file sent by the accounts department periodically."

My read

The need is valid, and it lands squarely on the second structural finding: there is no file import anywhere in this application. So this isn't "add an importer to invoices". It's "build the first import in the system", and a first one pays for everything a first one needs — upload handling, size and type limits, parse-and-preview, per-row validation with errors a clerk can act on, idempotency so a file sent twice doesn't pay everything twice, and an audit record of who imported what.

And the parsing isn't the hard part. Matching is. A payment file from Accounts identifies invoices by their reference, not ours — and that one join is where imports like this usually die, late.

I'd also challenge the direction

If Accounts is the system of record for payments, the cheaper and more reliable integration usually runs the other way: we export our payables — we already compute the aging — they pay, and they send back something much smaller. Invoice reference, date paid, amount. Insisting on consuming their full file means we track their format changes forever, and we won't be told when it changes.

What has to be settled first

Agree the file contract with Accounts — three columns and a reference both sides accept — then build a narrow importer for exactly that. Don't build a general import framework on the strength of one requester; STAFF2 is already queueing behind it.

Integrate Zoho CRM instead of holding devotees here

"We should plan to integrate with another application (ZOHO CRM) which is managing the devotee database and service allocations. No need for duplicating this feature."

My read

The most consequential comment in the document, and it deserves a firm answer rather than a polite one.

First, a fact worth putting in front of them. ERPNext + Zoho CRM + Power BI was formally evaluated for this project and rejected — it's recorded in CHANGELOG.md:303. This isn't new ground, and the reviewers should know it was walked once already.

Second, on the merits: we already agree, and the screen reflects it. Our devotee page has no create form, deliberately. Devotees register themselves, choose their temple, and consent for themselves — because an admin creating an account on somebody's behalf produced a person who had agreed to nothing and contact details nobody had confirmed. There's no role control either, because a devotee holds one role by definition. What's left is the single decision an admin genuinely makes: whether this person may still sign in. That's not a CRM and never wanted to be.

Third: what would we actually pull? Our devotee record exists for one reason — to sign in and take a volunteer shift. Identity comes from Firebase, which proves somebody controls an email or a phone number. Authorisation comes from our own users table, deliberately, because a token proves control of an address and nothing more.

A Zoho record can supply neither. The integration would import contact details we don't need, for people who must still register before they can sign in — and we'd own a sync forever.

Fourth, the part worth taking seriously, and it isn't the part they emphasised. If Zoho is genuinely where volunteer service is allocated today, then our shift signups are a second place, and that is duplication. But it runs the opposite way from the one they named: the answer would be to stop allocating service here — a scope conversation about Epic 7 — not to import contact details.

The fix

Put one question back to them precisely: is Zoho where volunteer service is allocated today, and would the temple stop doing it there? If yes, that's a scope decision for Rajeev about Epic 7. If no, decline, and record the second refusal beside the first.

STAFF1

Remarks, to record behaviour

"Need remarks to record their behaviours."

My read

Valid gap, and the obvious fix is the wrong one.

We have staff_profiles.notes — an unlabelled TEXT, presented as a single-line input labelled "Notes", with no author, no date, no history, overwritable by anyone holding MANAGE_STAFF.

Using that to record conduct is worse than not recording it. It's an employment record about a real person, nobody knows who wrote it or when, and the next edit silently destroys what was there.

What's needed is a dated, attributed, append-only staff note — author, timestamp, body. That shape exists here already: make_append_only() on stock movements, and the audit trail. Append-only isn't fussiness; an employment note that can be edited after the fact is worth nothing on the day it matters.

Two things to settle before building

  • Access. Conduct notes aren't general staff data. Today anyone with MANAGE_STAFF sees everything on a profile. A conduct note needs its own permission and probably its own visibility rule, or the first thing that happens is a kitchen manager reading their colleague's warning.
  • Scope. "Behaviours" slides into appraisals, ratings and formal warnings very fast. Build the dated note and nothing else, and refuse the rating field until somebody can say what reads it.

One flag: we already hold a cross-temple employment ban mechanism. A conduct note must not quietly become an input to a ban without an explicit decision to make it one.

STAFF2

Import salaries and advances from Finance

"Periodic data import from the finance department for the salaries, advances, etc."

My read

Same structural answer as INVOICE1 — no import exists — plus a scope question that matters more than the plumbing.

We hold staff_profiles.monthly_salary and we encrypt PAN. We hold no advances, no deductions, no payslips, no pay ledger, and nothing in Phase 1 says we should. "Import salaries and advances" is really "become a partial payroll system".

And a partial payroll record is one people will trust. If we hold an advance Finance changed last week and our file is a month old, somebody reads a stale number off our screen and acts on it.

So the question is what the data is for, on our screens:

  • To show a staff member their own pay? That's employee self-service, and Finance's own system almost certainly does it better.
  • To cost meals with labour? E3-S8 D4 already says labour can only be allocated, not measured, and a salary figure doesn't change that.
  • "So it's all in one place"? The weakest of the three reasons to hold sensitive pay data — and the data-protection cost is real rather than theoretical. Salary and advances sitting beside an encrypted PAN in one row raises the stakes of any leak.

The fix

Decline until somebody can name the reader. If it does go ahead: reuse whatever importer INVOICE1 builds, keep it read-only, and stamp every figure "as supplied by Finance on <date>" so nobody mistakes it for live.

SS1

A heatmap of where the gaps are

"Along with the tables, we should have a heatmap like picture highlighting the gaps for the week/day/month. This is much more usable than a table of data."

My read

The sharpest comment in the document, and the reason it's right is more specific than "a heatmap is nicer to look at".

The schedule screen shows supply only. A seven-column table of who works which hours, with one footer row: "In that day — N staff, M volunteers". Nowhere does it show what was needed. And we compute need — MealCrewService knows how many people a meal takes and how many are rostered, and it warns on the planner when crew.rostered < crewRequired.

Both halves of the answer exist in the system and have never been put on the same screen. Today a gap is visible only to somebody who happens to open the right meal on the planner.

That's the fix, and it isn't really a heatmap. It's coverage against requirement on the schedule grid — each day carrying its shortfall, and how many people short. The distinction isn't pedantry: a heatmap of raw headcount would be decorative, and our design system's rule is that status colour is never decorative. Colour by shortfall passes that rule. Colour by "how many people are in" fails it, because a busy day and a well-staffed day would look identical.

I'd resist the month view to begin with. A month of days by staff is a wall, and the question being asked — where am I short? — is answered better by a compact week strip plus a "next thirty days, the days you are short" list than by thirty columns of colour.

The fix

A new aggregate endpoint — need against rostered, per day — and a rework of the grid's colour and its footer. The data is already there.
